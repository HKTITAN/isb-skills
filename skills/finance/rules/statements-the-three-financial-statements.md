**The Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position)**

A snapshot at a specific moment showing what the company owns and how it's financed.

Structure:
- **Assets:** Listed from most liquid (cash) to least liquid (buildings)
  - Current assets: Cash, accounts receivable, inventory (convertible within 12 months)
  - Non-current assets: PP&E, intangibles, long-term investments
- **Liabilities:** Listed by urgency
  - Current liabilities: Accounts payable, short-term debt, accrued expenses
  - Non-current liabilities: Long-term debt, pension obligations
- **Equity:** Contributed capital plus retained earnings

Key metrics:
- **Working Capital** = Current Assets – Current Liabilities
- **Net Tangible Assets** = Total Assets – Intangible Assets – Liabilities

**The Income Statement (Profit & Loss Statement)**

Covers a period and shows profitability.

Structure:
```
Revenue (top line)
- COGS (cost of goods sold)
= Gross Profit
- Operating expenses (R&D, SG&A)
= Operating Income (EBIT)
- Interest expense
- Taxes
= Net Income (bottom line)
```

Key margins:
- Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue
- Operating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue  
- Net Profit Margin = Net Income / Revenue

**The Cash Flow Statement**

Shows actual cash movement; critical because Net Income ≠ Cash.

Three sections:

*Operating Activities*: Cash from core business
```
Net Income
+ Depreciation/Amortization (non-cash)
- Increase in receivables (cash not yet collected)
+ Increase in payables (deferred cash outflow)
= Operating Cash Flow
```

*Investing Activities*: CapEx, acquisitions, asset sales
- Usually negative (companies investing in growth)

*Financing Activities*: Debt, equity, dividends
- Shows how company finances itself

**Free Cash Flow (FCF)** = Operating Cash Flow - CapEx
- Most important metric: cash available to all investors (equity + debt)
- Must be positive long-term to be sustainable
