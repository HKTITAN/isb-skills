**MM Proposition I (No Taxes):** Firm value is independent of capital structure.

The market values operating cash flows, not how they're financed. Leverage benefits equity holders, but increases their risk proportionally, offsetting the benefit.

**MM Proposition II:** Cost of equity rises with leverage to offset cheaper cost of debt.

```
Cost of Equity = Unlevered Cost + (Unlevered - Cost of Debt) × (Debt/Equity)

Example:
Unlevered cost: 10%, Cost of Debt: 6%, D/E: 0.5
Cost of Equity = 10% + (10% - 6%) × 0.5 = 10% + 2% = 12%

If you increase D/E to 1.0:
Cost of Equity = 10% + (10% - 6%) × 1.0 = 10% + 4% = 14%
```

**With Taxes (realistic):** Interest is tax-deductible, creating **tax shield**.

```
Tax Shield Value = Debt × Tax Rate × Cost of Debt (perpetual)
Or annually: Interest Expense × Tax Rate

Example: $100M debt, 6% rate, 25% tax rate
Annual tax shield: $100M × 6% × 25% = $1.5M
Perpetual value: $1.5M / 6% = $25M
```

This is free value from leverage; explains why companies use debt.
