DuPont breaks ROE into drivers, revealing performance sources.

**3-Factor DuPont:**
```
ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
ROE = (NI/Revenue) × (Revenue/Assets) × (Assets/Equity)
```

**Example:**
```
Company financials:
Net Income: $100M
Revenue: $1,000M
Total Assets: $500M
Shareholders' Equity: $250M

Calculations:
Net Profit Margin = $100M / $1,000M = 10%
Asset Turnover = $1,000M / $500M = 2.0x
Equity Multiplier = $500M / $250M = 2.0x

ROE = 10% × 2.0 × 2.0 = 40%
```

Interpretation:
- **10% Net Margin:** Company keeps 10 cents per revenue dollar
- **2.0x Asset Turnover:** Each dollar of assets generates $2 of revenue
- **2.0x Equity Multiplier:** Assets financed 50% by debt (leverage)

The 40% ROE comes from:
- Healthy profitability (10% margin)
- Efficient asset use (2.0x turnover)
- Moderate leverage (2.0x multiplier)

**5-Factor Extended DuPont:**
```
ROE = Tax Burden × Interest Burden × EBIT Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier

Where:
- Tax Burden = NI / EBT [effect of taxes]
- Interest Burden = EBT / EBIT [effect of debt interest]
- EBIT Margin = EBIT / Revenue [core operating profitability]
- Asset Turnover = Revenue / Assets [efficiency]
- Equity Multiplier = Assets / Equity [leverage]
```

Reveals whether ROE improvement comes from operations, financing, or taxes.

**Using DuPont for Analysis:**

Two companies with identical 15% ROE but different drivers:

Company A: 10% margin × 1.0 turnover × 1.5 leverage = 15%
- High profitability, slow-growing, moderate debt

Company B: 5% margin × 2.0 turnover × 1.5 leverage = 15%
- Thin-margin, high-volume (like fast food), same leverage

Same ROE but very different businesses:
- Company A vulnerable if margins compress
- Company B vulnerable if volume slows
- Both equally leveraged, but different risks
