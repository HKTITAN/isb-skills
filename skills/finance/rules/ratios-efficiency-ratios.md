**Asset Turnover** = Revenue / Average Total Assets

How many dollars of sales per dollar of assets?

Example:
```
Revenue: $500M, Average Assets: $100M
Turnover = 5x (each $1 of assets generates $5 of sales)

Tech/SaaS: 4-8x (asset-light)
Retail: 1-3x (inventory-heavy)
Real Estate: 0.3-0.8x (capital-intensive)
```

**Inventory Turnover** = COGS / Average Inventory

How fast does inventory convert to sales?

Example:
```
COGS: $200M, Average Inventory: $40M
Turnover = 5x per year (every 73 days)

Grocery: 15x+ (fast-moving)
Apparel: 4-6x
Luxury goods: 1-2x
```

**Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO)** = 365 / Inventory Turnover

```
DIO = 365 / 5 = 73 days
On average, inventory sits 73 days before sale
Lower = better (less cash tied up)
```

**Accounts Receivable Turnover** = Revenue / Average AR

```
Example: Revenue $1B, Average AR $200M
Turnover = 5x (collect receivables 5 times per year)
```

**Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)** = 365 / AR Turnover

```
DSO = 365 / 5 = 73 days
On average, takes 73 days to collect from customers

B2B typical: 30-60 days
B2C: same-day or <7 days
Rising DSO: Red flag (collection problems or credit deterioration)
```

**Accounts Payable Turnover** = COGS / Average AP

**Days Payable Outstanding (DPO)** = 365 / AP Turnover

Average days before paying suppliers.

```
DPO: 40 days (take 40 days to pay suppliers)
High DPO: Improves cash, damages supplier relationships
```

**Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)** = DSO + DIO - DPO

Days of working capital tied up in the business.

Example:
```
DSO: 45 days, DIO: 60 days, DPO: 40 days
CCC = 45 + 60 - 40 = 65 days

Company needs to finance 65 days of operations
Reduction in CCC = working capital released
Every 10-day reduction × daily cash burn = cash freed
```
