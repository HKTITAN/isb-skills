Blended cost of debt + equity; used as DCF discount rate.

**Formula:**
```
WACC = (E/V) × Re + (D/V) × Rd × (1 - Tax Rate)

Where:
- E = Market value of equity (stock price × shares)
- D = Market value of debt
- V = E + D (total value)
- Re = Cost of equity (from CAPM)
- Rd = Pre-tax cost of debt
- Tax Rate = Corporate income tax rate
```

**Step-by-Step Calculation:**

```
Company ABC:
Market Cap (E): $1,000M (stock: $50, shares: 20M)
Total Debt (D): $400M
Total Value (V): $1,400M

Weights:
E/V = $1,000M / $1,400M = 71.4%
D/V = $400M / $1,400M = 28.6%

Cost of Equity (Re):
Rf = 6%, β = 1.1, MRP = 7%
Re = 6% + 1.1 × 7% = 13.7%

Cost of Debt (Pre-tax):
Interest expense: $32M
Rd = $32M / $400M = 8%

After-tax Cost of Debt:
Tax rate: 25%
Rd × (1 - T) = 8% × 0.75 = 6%

WACC Calculation:
WACC = 0.714 × 13.7% + 0.286 × 6% = 9.78% + 1.72% = 11.5%
```

Interpretation: Company must earn 11.5% on capital to satisfy both equity and debt holders.

**Key Insights:**

- Use **market values**, not book values (market cap, market value of debt)
- **Tax shield on debt** (Rd × (1-T)) makes debt cheaper than it appears
- Higher leverage → Lower WACC (cheaper debt) but higher financial risk
- WACC increases once bankruptcy risk becomes material
- Sensitivity: 1% change in WACC changes valuation by 10-15%
