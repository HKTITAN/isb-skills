Consumers use mental shortcuts (heuristics) that often work but sometimes create errors:

**Availability bias**: Overweight easily recalled information
- Recent good experience inflates perception of restaurant quality
- Memorable bad service makes brand seem worse than it is

**Anchoring**: Influenced by first number seen
- "Was $100, now $50" seems better value than just "$50"
- Original price anchor influences perception

**Social proof**: Do what others do
- Positive reviews signal quality
- Full restaurant seems good; empty restaurant seems bad
- Laugh tracks make jokes seem funnier

**Loss aversion**: Feel losses more strongly than equivalent gains
- Losing $50 hurts more than finding $50 feels good
- Makes people reluctant to try new options (fear of losing familiar quality)

**Sunk cost fallacy**: Continue investing because already invested
- "I paid for gym membership, so I should use it" even if you hate it
- Shouldn't matter but influences decisions

Using these insights in marketing:
- Use social proof (reviews, testimonials, customer counts) to overcome doubt
- Use anchoring (show original price before discount) to create value perception
- Reduce perceived loss (free trials, 30-day guarantees) to overcome loss aversion
- Keep communication frequent (recent visits stay top-of-mind due to availability)
