#### Price Discrimination & Segmentation

Charge different prices to different customer segments based on willingness to pay.

**First-degree price discrimination**: Charge each customer maximum they're willing to pay
- Requires perfect information about valuations
- Rare in practice; mostly through negotiation (business contracts, consulting)
- Risk: Customers discover others paid less and feel cheated

**Second-degree price discrimination**: Offer different price/quality bundles; customers self-select
- Software with Starter (cheap, limited) / Professional (mid-price, more features) / Enterprise (expensive, all features) tiers
- Customers choose bundle matching their value
- Maximizes revenue by serving different segments profitably

**Third-degree price discrimination**: Charge different prices to different groups
- Student discounts, senior discounts, off-peak pricing, geographic pricing
- Airlines charge business travelers more than leisure travelers
- Movie theaters price by time of day

Price discrimination increases profits by capturing customer surplus. But it creates backlash if customers feel treated unfairly. Perceived fairness and transparency matter.

#### Dynamic Pricing

Adjust prices in real-time based on demand, supply, competition, and customer characteristics.

How it works:
- Raise prices when demand exceeds supply
- Lower prices when supply exceeds demand
- Optimize for maximum revenue: (Price × Expected Volume) - Costs

Industries using dynamic pricing:
- **Airlines**: Peak season, peak times, advance bookings cost more. Last-minute availability drops prices.
- **Hotels**: Peak season rates triple; off-season rates half. Dynamic by occupancy level.
- **Ride-sharing**: Surge pricing during peak demand (11 PM Friday, rush hour)
- **E-commerce**: Amazon adjusts prices millions of times daily based on inventory, competition, demand
- **Restaurants**: Premium pricing during peak hours; discounts for off-peak

Pros:
- Revenue and profit optimization
- Reduces waste (empty seats, unsold inventory)
- Enables price-sensitive customers to access at lower price

Cons:
- **Fairness concerns**: Customers paying different prices for identical products feels unfair
- **Discrimination risk**: Algorithms may inadvertently proxy for protected characteristics
- **Information asymmetry**: Prices change faster than customers can monitor
- **Privacy concerns**: Requires tracking customer behavior for personalization
- **Backlash**: Surge pricing during emergencies feels exploitative

#### Bundling

Combine multiple products/services into a single package at one price.

Why bundling works:
- Customers have heterogeneous preferences
- Some value Product A highly but value Product B weakly (and vice versa)
- Bundling captures more value from both customer types than selling separately

Example: Cable companies bundle TV, internet, phone. Customer might value TV highly but internet less. Without bundling, they'd buy only TV. With bundling, they buy all three at combined price that's attractive to both types.

Benefits:
- Captures more customer surplus
- Simplifies choice
- Increases perceived fairness (getting more for one price)
- Reduces complexity (fewer SKUs, pricing tiers)

#### Psychological Pricing

How customers perceive prices influences demand:

- **Price points**: $9.99 feels cheaper than $10.00, even though it's 1 cent more
- **Prestige pricing**: Luxury goods priced high to signal exclusivity and quality
- **Reference pricing**: Customers anchor to previous price ("Was $100, now $50" feels like better value than just "$50")
- **Loss aversion**: People feel losses more than gains; "Risk-free guarantee" reduces perceived loss
- **Charm pricing**: .99 endings trigger emotional rather than rational processing
