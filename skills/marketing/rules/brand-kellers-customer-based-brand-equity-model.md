Customer-based brand equity (CBBE) is the differential effect of brand knowledge on customer response to marketing. In simpler terms: Does the brand name change how customers perceive, feel about, and act toward the product?

Example: A bottle of water labeled "Evian" costs 5x more than identical water from an unlabeled bottle. The premium is brand equity.

**Keller's Brand Pyramid: Four Levels of Brand Building**

Brand building proceeds from foundation to apex:

#### Level 1: Salience (Brand Awareness)

Salience is whether and how easily customers recall and recognize the brand.

Two dimensions:
- **Recognition**: "Have you heard of this brand?" (aided recall from seeing logo/name)
- **Recall**: "What brands in this category can you name?" (unaided recall, spontaneous)
- **Top-of-mind**: Brand recalled first when category is mentioned (most valuable)

Why salience matters:
- Without awareness, no consideration
- Top-of-mind awareness drives trial and purchase
- Salience is necessary but not sufficient for high equity

Building salience:
- Consistent, memorable advertising and media presence
- Distinctive naming, logo, visual identity
- Sponsorships, word-of-mouth, social proof
- Ubiquitous distribution (high availability)

Examples:
- Nike's swoosh is highly salient in athletic footwear
- Coca-Cola is instantly recognized globally; packaging is iconic
- Unknown indie brands must work harder to gain initial consideration

#### Level 2: Performance and Imagery

**Performance** is how well the brand delivers on functional attributes—product features, quality, reliability, consistency.

Performance examples:
- Apple: Intuitive interfaces, ecosystem integration, durability
- Samsung: Innovation in display technology, feature richness
- BMW: Engineering quality, performance, driving dynamics

**Imagery** is non-functional, psychologically-driven associations—what the brand means emotionally or socially to customers.

Imagery includes:
- Brand personality: Human characteristics (sincere, exciting, competent, sophisticated, rugged)
- User imagery: "What kind of person uses this brand?" (athlete, creative, professional)
- Usage imagery: Situations/occasions for use (celebration, everyday, adventure)
- Emotional associations: How does brand make you feel? (trust, excitement, belonging, status)
- Values alignment: Does brand align with customer values? (environmental, social, ethical)
- Heritage and aspirations: Brand history, legacy, growth trajectory

Imagery examples:
- Apple: Creativity, innovation, premium lifestyle, minimalism
- Nike: Athletic excellence, determination, pushing limits, "just do it" attitude
- Luxury brands: Status, sophistication, exclusivity, heritage

Both performance and imagery matter. A luxury brand with poor performance (breaks easily) loses equity. A functional product without positive imagery struggles to command premium pricing.

#### Level 3: Judgments and Feelings

**Judgments** are customer evaluations of the brand:
- Overall quality perception relative to alternatives
- Credibility: expertise (does brand know what it's doing?), trustworthiness, likability
- Uniqueness: is brand different and distinctive?
- Consideration: would I actually consider this brand?

**Feelings** are emotional responses to the brand:
- Trust and security
- Fun and excitement
- Social approval and status
- Self-esteem and confidence
- Belonging and community
- Inspiration and aspiration

Feelings are often more powerful drivers of loyalty than rational judgments. A customer might rationally know Brand A is superior, but feels better using Brand B emotionally.

Building judgments and feelings:
- Deliver on brand promise consistently
- Brand storytelling that creates emotional connection
- Authenticity and transparency build trust
- Social proof (reviews, testimonials, customer counts) overcome skepticism
- Community building fosters belonging
- Values alignment creates resonance

#### Level 4: Resonance (Loyalty & Advocacy)

Resonance is the deepest level of brand relationship—emotional bonding, loyalty, and willingness to pay premium prices.

Resonance indicators:
- **Repeat purchase**: Consistent brand choice
- **Loyalty**: Resistance to competitive offers, willingness to seek brand out
- **Advocacy**: Word-of-mouth, recommendations, reviews
- **Willingness to pay premium**: Price insensitivity within reason
- **Brand community**: Sense of belonging to group of users (Apple fans, Harley riders)
- **Engagement**: Participation in brand experiences and communities

Why resonance matters:
- Generates sustained revenue and profit
- Reduces customer acquisition costs (word-of-mouth)
- Creates competitive moat (hard to displace loyal customers)
- Enables new product extensions (loyal customers try new offerings)
- Increases customer lifetime value

Example: iPhone users often buy Apple Watches, iPads, Macs, and services because of resonance. Loyalty extends across entire product ecosystem.
