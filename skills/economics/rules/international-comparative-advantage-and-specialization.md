**Opportunity Cost Framework**
- Comparative advantage: Lower opportunity cost than competitors
- Specialization: Each country produces good with lowest opportunity cost
- Trade gains: Both countries can consume more by specializing and trading
- Distribution: Trade creates winners (sectors with comparative advantage) and losers (import-competing sectors)

**Factor Endowments and Trade Patterns**
- Heckscher-Ohlin: Capital-abundant countries export capital-intensive goods; labor-abundant export labor-intensive
- Examples: USA exports machinery, chemicals; Bangladesh exports textiles, apparel
- Income distribution: Capital owners gain in capital-rich countries; workers gain in labor-rich countries
- Dynamic: Product life cycle shows products shift manufacturing to lower-cost locations as they mature
