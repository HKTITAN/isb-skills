**Economic vs. Accounting Profit**
- Accounting profit: Revenue - Accounting costs
- Economic profit: Revenue - (Accounting costs + Opportunity costs)
- Opportunity cost: Cost of capital and foregone opportunities
- Normal profit: Zero economic profit; just compensates investors for risk
- Economic rents: Returns exceeding normal profit; indicates competitive advantage

**Sources of Economic Rents (Barriers to Entry)**
1. Scale economies: Efficient scale large relative to market; only one firm fits (utilities, software platforms)
2. Switching costs: Customers locked in; moving requires retooling (enterprise software, banking)
3. Intellectual property: Patents, copyrights exclude competitors (pharmaceuticals, technology)
4. Control of scarce resources: Unique inputs competitors can't access (De Beers diamonds, OPEC oil)
5. Network effects: Value increases with users; hard to displace incumbent (social networks, OS, payment systems)
6. High capital requirements: Entry capital prohibitively large (auto manufacturing, nuclear power)
7. Regulatory barriers: FCC licenses, casino licenses, airline routes

**Sustainable Competitive Advantage**
- Erosion risk: Economic profits attract entry; competition erodes advantages
- Dynamic defense: Must continuously innovate, invest in moats, adapt to changing environment
- First-mover advantage: Establishing dominance early (switching costs, network effects) creates lasting advantage
- Imitation barriers: Difficult-to-copy advantages (brand, organizational culture, patents) most durable
