**Anchoring and Adjustment**
- Anchoring: Initial information (even arbitrary) disproportionately influences decisions
- Adjustment: Adjustments from anchor typically insufficient; people don't move far enough
- Application: Negotiation anchors (first offer strongly influences final price)
- Numerical anchors: Random numbers influence price estimates, salary negotiations, damage awards
- Psychological mechanism: Number serves as starting point; full adjustment to true value requires effort

**Availability Heuristic**
- Definition: Judging probability/frequency by how easily examples come to mind
- Bias: Recent, vivid, or personally experienced events seem more common than statistical base rates
- Media coverage: Dramatic events (plane crashes) seem more likely than statistically more probable risks
- Application: Marketing uses vivid examples to increase product salience and perceived desirability
- Business impact: Brands with memorable advertising appear more popular than they are

**Representativeness Heuristic**
- Definition: Judging probability by similarity to typical example of category
- Bias: Ignores base rates, sample size, and regression to mean
- Conjunction fallacy: "Linda is feminist bank teller" judged more likely than "Linda is bank teller" (ignores base rate)
- Application: Stereotyping affects hiring, lending, customer targeting decisions
- Overconfidence: Entrepreneurs overestimate success probability (ignoring base rates; ~90% fail)

**Framing Effects**
- Gain frame vs loss frame: Same choice presented as gain or loss produces different decisions
- Attribute framing: Emphasizing positive attributes (95% fat-free) more persuasive than negative (5% fat)
- Goal framing: Emphasizing benefits vs risks changes willingness to engage in behavior
- Reference point shifts: Framing redefines reference point, changing losses and gains
- Classic example: Asian Disease Problem (72% choose certain option in gain frame; 22% in equivalent loss frame)

**Status Quo Bias**
- Definition: Tendency to maintain current state; inaction default
- Causes: Loss aversion (changing means losses loom large), switching costs, endowment effect
- Power of defaults: Default pension enrollment, health insurance choice, organ donation
- Magnitude: Default option chosen by 70-90% of people despite identical alternatives
- Overcoming: Reduce friction, highlight benefits of change, make switching easy

**Endowment Effect**
- Definition: Valuation of owned item exceeds willingness to pay if didn't own it
- Mechanism: Ownership triggers loss aversion; selling triggers loss frame
- Evidence: Mug experiment (owners ask higher prices than buyers willing to pay, ~2:1 ratio)
- Business implication: Free trials leverage endowment effect to increase willingness to buy
- Labor markets: Workers demand higher pay to move jobs (endowed current position)

**Mental Accounting**
- Definition: Individuals categorize finances (spending, savings) into separate mental accounts
- Bracketing: Evaluating outcomes individually vs together changes preferences
- Cost allocation: Sunk costs should be ignored but psychologically weigh heavily
- Application: Consumers treat "found money" differently than earned income (higher propensity to spend)
- Gift cards: Channel spending into specific mental account; people spend more than equivalent cash
- Bonuses: Treated differently than salary; workers value bonuses more despite fungible money

**Hyperbolic Discounting**
- Definition: Discount rate decreases over time; immediate rewards heavily favored
- Present bias: Overvalue immediate gratification relative to future
- Preference reversals: Prefer smaller-sooner reward when both are far future; but immediate reward when "now" option available
- Application: Gym memberships (overestimate future workout commitment); procrastination; addiction
- Solution: Commitment devices (prepayment, automatic transfers) help overcome present bias

**Overconfidence**
- Definition: Believe own knowledge, abilities, and predictions more accurate than warranted
- Manifestations: Overestimate probability of success, underestimate risk, believe uniquely positioned
- Biased self-assessment: Majority believe above-average managers; most investors think they beat market (90% of drivers think above-average)
- Corporate applications: CEOs overconfident about M&A success; entrepreneurs overestimate business viability
- Impact: Poor decision-making, excessive risk-taking, inadequate due diligence

**Confirmation Bias**
- Definition: Seek information confirming existing beliefs and ignore contradicting information
- Manifestations: Investors hold losing stocks hoping for recovery; sell winners (confirming loss)
- Business impact: Organizations resist data contradicting strategy; entrepreneurs cling to failing pivots
- Effect on decisions: Make initial anchors more sticky; hard to dislodge from first impression
