**Comparative Advantage**
- Ricardo principle: Countries specialize in goods with lowest opportunity cost
- Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF): Each country produces bundle of two goods; trade allows consumption beyond PPF
- Gains from trade: All countries benefit from specialization and trade
- Pattern of specialization: Determined by comparative advantage; depends on relative factor endowments, technology, preferences

**Terms of Trade**
- Definition: Ratio of export prices to import prices; measures trading position
- Improvement: Export prices rise relative to import prices; less exports needed to buy same imports
- Deterioration: Export prices fall relative to import prices; more exports needed to buy same imports
- Resource curse: Countries with natural resources often face long-run terms of trade deterioration
- Income distribution: Terms of trade changes affect income distribution between exporters and importers

**Trade Policy**
- Tariffs: Tax on imports; raises import prices, protects domestic producers, reduces consumer surplus
- Quotas: Quantity restriction on imports; similar effects to tariffs
- Subsidies: Government payment to domestic producers; raises output, reduces imports, hurts foreign competitors
- Strategic protection: Protecting infant industries with high growth potential; learning-by-doing justifies temporary protection
- Distributional effects: Protection benefits import-competing producers but harms consumers and export-competing sectors

**Exchange Rate Determination**
- Purchasing Power Parity (PPP): Exchange rate should equalize price levels across countries
- Deviations: PPP doesn't hold short-run due to sticky prices, different consumption baskets, transportation costs
- Interest rate parity: Risk-free rate differentials equal expected currency appreciation
- Expectations: If expect currency appreciation, demand rises, currency appreciates immediately
- Overshooting: Exchange rates overshoot long-run equilibrium in short-run due to faster adjustment than goods prices

**Capital Flows and Balance of Payments**
- Current account deficit: Country imports more than exports; requires offsetting capital inflow
- Capital inflow: Foreign investment in domestic assets (stocks, bonds, FDI)
- Sustainability: Large current deficits require willing foreign investors; if confidence lost, crisis
- Financial constraints: Developing countries with poor credit ratings face high borrowing costs
- Contagion: Crisis in one country can spread through capital flow reversals
