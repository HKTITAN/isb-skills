**Payoff Matrices and Strategic Interaction**
- Payoff matrix: Shows payoffs for each player for each combination of strategies
- Strategy: Complete plan of action for all contingencies
- Pure strategy: Deterministic choice; always play specific action
- Mixed strategy: Probabilistic choice; randomize among actions

**Dominant Strategies**
- Definition: Strategy that yields highest payoff regardless of what opponent does
- Dominant strategy equilibrium: All players have dominant strategies
- Weak dominance: Strategy yields at least as good payoff, strictly better in some cases
- Elimination of dominated strategies: Rational players never play dominated strategies
- Not always present: Many games lack dominant strategies; outcome depends on opponent's move

**Nash Equilibrium**
- Definition: Strategy combination where no player can improve payoff by unilaterally changing strategy
- Self-enforcing: Each player's strategy is best response to others' strategies
- Not always optimal: Nash equilibrium may be worse for all players than alternative outcomes (Prisoner's Dilemma)
- Finding equilibrium: Identify best response function for each player; find intersection
- Multiple equilibria: Some games have multiple Nash equilibria (coordination games)

**Prisoner's Dilemma**
- Setup: Two prisoners choose cooperate or defect; mutual cooperation best outcome, but both have incentive to defect
- Outcome: Both defect (dominant strategy) despite mutual cooperation being Pareto superior
- Business examples: Price wars, R&D races, pollution, advertising arms races
- Resolution: Repeated games enable cooperation through reputation and punishment; transparency enables detection

**Coordination Games**
- Setup: Players benefit from choosing same action but may have different preferences over which action
- Multiple Nash equilibria: Several equally valid but different equilibrium outcomes
- Examples: Technology standards (VHS vs Betamax), network effects (Facebook vs Myspace)
- First-mover advantage: Early mover can establish dominant standard, making coordination on that standard
- Network effects amplify: More users on platform increase value; positive feedback locks in leaders
