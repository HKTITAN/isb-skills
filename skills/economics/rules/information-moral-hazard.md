**Definition and Mechanism**
- Moral hazard: Incentive misalignment after contract causes one party to take excessive risk at other's expense
- Hidden action: Agent's effort/care not directly observable after contract signed
- Risk shift: After insured, person's incentive to prevent loss diminishes; insurance creates moral hazard

**Insurance Moral Hazard**
- Fire insurance: Insured person less vigilant about fire prevention
- Health insurance: Insured person consumes more healthcare, takes more health risks
- Auto insurance: Insured person drives less carefully, takes more risks
- Effect on insurer: Higher loss rates than actuarially estimated, profit erosion

**Solutions to Moral Hazard**
- Deductibles/coinsurance: Make insured person bear part of loss, restoring prevention incentives
- No-claim discounts: Reward zero-loss periods, incentivizing care
- Exclusions: Deny coverage for grossly negligent acts
- Verification: Claims investigation, accident reconstruction, fraud detection
- Monitoring: Technology (GPS, cameras, software tracking) reduces moral hazard

**Employment Moral Hazard (Principal-Agent Problem)**
- Principal-agent: Employer (principal) wants worker effort, but cannot fully observe work
- Shirking: Worker may exert less effort than contracted; hidden action
- Employer profit erosion: Hidden underperformance reduces productivity and outcomes
- Worker incentive: Shirking increases with lower wage, worse job security, easier monitoring evasion

**Agency Cost Solutions**
- Wage incentives: Piece rates, bonuses, stock options align worker interest with firm value
- Monitoring: Supervision, performance metrics, employee monitoring software
- Reputation: Long-term employment deters shirking (threat of job loss)
- Team competition: Peer pressure and comparison reduce shirking
- Monitoring cost-benefit: Monitoring is expensive; in some jobs, high-powered incentives dominate
- Relationship value: Long-term relationships create mutual investment in good outcomes
