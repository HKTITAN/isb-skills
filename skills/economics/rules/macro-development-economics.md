**Solow Growth Model**
- Framework: Output grows from capital accumulation (K), labor growth (L), human capital (H), and technology (A)
- Capital accumulation equation: dK/dt = sY − δK (investment minus depreciation)
- Per capita terms: dk/dt = s·f(k) − (n + g + δ)k (per capita capital changes when investment exceeds capital widening)

**Steady State and Convergence**
- Steady state: Capital per worker reaches level where investment = depreciation
  - No further growth in capital per capita
  - Output per capita grows at exogenous technological growth rate g only
- Convergence hypothesis: Poor countries with low capital catch up to rich countries
  - Unconditional convergence: All countries converge to same steady state (not observed)
  - Conditional convergence: Countries converge to their own steady state, determined by:
    - Saving rate
    - Education/human capital investment
    - Institutional quality (rule of law, property rights)
    - Infrastructure quality

**Poverty Trap Mechanics**
- Mechanism: Low income → Low savings → Low capital → Low productivity → Low income (circular)
- Causes:
  - Low savings: Poor people consume all income; can't accumulate capital
  - Low human capital: Families can't afford education for children
  - Poor health: Malnutrition, disease reduce productivity
  - Broken institutions: Weak property rights discourage investment
  - External dependence: High debt obligations, unfavorable trade terms
  - Low innovation: Brain drain prevents technological catch-up

**Escaping Poverty Traps**
- Big push hypothesis: Coordinated investment across sectors can break trap
  - Infrastructure improvements lower business costs for all
  - Education expansion creates skilled workforce
  - Health improvements increase productivity
  - Creates positive feedback loop
- Escape factors:
  - Strong political will and leadership
  - Educational investment (especially female education)
  - Health improvements (mortality decline precedes income growth)
  - Institutional reforms (property rights, rule of law)
  - Export-led growth strategy (access world markets)

**Institutions and Growth**
- Institutional quality determines: Investment incentives, contract enforcement, corruption limits, public good provision
- Extractive vs. inclusive institutions:
  - Extractive: Power concentrated, no rule of law; grows slowly despite resources
  - Inclusive: Broad property rights, open markets; enables sustained growth
- Institutional change: Institutions sticky; historical patterns persist; breaking path dependence requires political upheaval
- Corruption impact: Each percentage point of corruption reduces growth 0.5-1% annually; compounds into vast gaps
