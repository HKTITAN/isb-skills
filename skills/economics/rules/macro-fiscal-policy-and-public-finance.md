**Government Budget and Multipliers**
- Revenue sources: Income tax, corporate tax, consumption taxes (VAT/GST), property tax, tariffs
- Spending categories: Wages, infrastructure, healthcare, education, interest payments, pensions, subsidies
- Multiplier mechanism: Government spending → Income → Consumer spending → Further spending
- Multiplier size: Larger with higher MPC (poorer households), lower imports, lower savings, spare capacity

**Automatic Stabilizers vs. Discretionary Policy**
- Automatic stabilizers: Features that reduce cycle impact without deliberate policy (unemployment benefits, progressive taxes)
- Discretionary: Deliberate spending/tax changes to stimulate or contract economy
- Timing issue: Lags mean stimulus may arrive after recovery; poorly timed stimulus causes problems
- Crowding out: Government borrowing raises interest rates; can displace private investment

**Debt Sustainability**
- Debt dynamics: Accumulates when deficit > 0; grows if interest rate > growth rate
- Debt-to-GDP: Key metric; sustainable if growth exceeds interest rate
- Unsustainable indicators: Primary deficit persistent, interest rates rising, markets losing confidence
- Default risk: Sovereign default when government can't/won't pay; contagion spreads to similar countries

**Tax Incidence and Behavioral Effects**
- Nominal vs. real: Who pays legally vs. who bears burden; determined by elasticity
- Salience: Visible taxes (gas tax) more effective than hidden taxes (embedded sales tax) at same rate
- Behavioral compliance: Social norms matter; framing affects response (refund vs. tax reduction)
- Optimal design: Broad base, low rate, minimal distortion; progressive taxation for equity
