**First-Mover Advantage**
- Definition: Ability to establish dominant position by moving first
- Sources: Build customer switching costs, control scarce resources, establish technology standards
- Risks: High cost of pioneering market, uncertain demand, technological risk
- Conditions: Stronger with network effects, high switching costs, patent protection

**Switching Costs**
- Definition: Cost customers incur to switch from one seller to another
- Types: Technical (incompatibility), financial (penalty), learning (training needed), search (cost to find alternative)
- Strategic importance: Higher switching costs reduce price competition, increase pricing power
- Examples: Enterprise software, telecommunications, financial institutions

**Network Effects**
- Definition: Value increases with number of users/participants
- Positive feedback: More users → More value → Attract more users (reinforcing cycle)
- Lock-in: Early leader hard to dislodge; large installed base creates dominance
- Examples: Social networks, payment systems, operating systems
- Winner-take-most: Markets with strong network effects often end in single dominant firm
