**Lemons Problem (Akerlof)**
- Setup: Buyers cannot distinguish high-quality from low-quality goods; symmetric pooling price
- Market unraveling: At pooling price, high-quality sellers exit (underpriced); buyers left with low-quality
- Outcome: Information asymmetry causes market failure; quality goods disappear
- Used car market: Used cars sell at discount because buyers fear "lemons"
- Insurance market: Mix of high and low-risk customers prices based on average; low-risk drop out

**Adverse Selection in Insurance**
- Problem: Low-risk drivers know they're safer, but insurer can't tell
- Equilibrium process: Insurer sets premium based on average risk; at this premium, low-risk drivers drop out (overpriced), high-risk drivers stay (underpriced)
- Adverse selection: Market fills with high-risk drivers; equilibrium premium rises; more low-risk exit (death spiral)
- Result: Insurance becomes unavailable or unaffordable for average risks

**Solutions to Adverse Selection**
- Screening: Medical exams, driving history records, credit checks
- Signaling: Age, driving record, educational credentials reveal low-risk status
- Pooling/mandate: Government requires purchase (auto insurance), spreads cost across risk types
- Certification: Third-party verification of quality (grades, testing, inspection)
- Relationship effects: Long-term relationships reduce information asymmetry

**Credit Markets and Lending**
- Bank problem: Borrowers with weak projects most willing to accept high interest rates
- Selection: Borrowers with strong projects find high rates unattractive, exit market
- Bank portfolio: Fills with risky loans; high default rates necessitate even higher rates (worse selection ensues)
- Credit rationing: Banks prefer turning down some applicants to raising rates for all, which worsens selection pool
- Solutions: Collateral requirements, relationship lending, covenant monitoring, differential pricing by risk
