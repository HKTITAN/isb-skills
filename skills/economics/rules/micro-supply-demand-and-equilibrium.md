**Demand Curve Properties**
- Price elasticity: Responsiveness of quantity demanded to price changes
  - Elastic (|elasticity| > 1): Quantity changes proportionately more than price; revenue falls when price rises
  - Inelastic (|elasticity| < 1): Quantity changes proportionately less; revenue rises when price rises
  - Unit elastic: Price and quantity changes exactly offset; revenue constant
- Income elasticity: Responsiveness to income changes
  - Normal goods (elasticity > 0): Buy more when income rises
  - Inferior goods (elasticity < 0): Buy less when income rises
- Cross-elasticity: Responsiveness to other goods' prices
  - Substitutes (positive): Demand increases when substitute price rises
  - Complements (negative): Demand decreases when complement price rises

**Supply Curve Properties**
- Price elasticity of supply: Responsiveness of quantity supplied to price changes
- Positive relationship: Higher prices justify higher production costs
- Long-run vs short-run: Supply more elastic long-run (can adjust capacity); inelastic short-run (fixed capacity)
- Shifts: Technology improvements, input prices, expectations shift entire curve

**Market Equilibrium**
- Price clears market: Quantity demanded = quantity supplied
- Shortage: Quantity demanded > supplied; prices rise
- Surplus: Quantity supplied > demanded; prices fall
- Equilibrium properties: Stable, self-correcting, no tendency to change

**Price Controls**
- Price floor (minimum): If binding (above equilibrium), creates surplus, excess supply
  - Example: Minimum wage causes unemployment (excess labor supply)
- Price ceiling (maximum): If binding (below equilibrium), creates shortage, excess demand
  - Example: Rent control causes housing shortage, reduced quality
