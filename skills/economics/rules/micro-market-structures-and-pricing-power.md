**Perfect Competition**
- Characteristics: Many buyers/sellers, homogeneous products, perfect information, free entry/exit
- Outcome: Price = marginal cost, firms earn normal profit (economic profit zero)
- Firm view: Price taker; perfectly elastic demand at market price
- Efficiency: Allocatively efficient (P=MC); no deadweight loss
- Reality: Rare; used as baseline to understand where rents come from

**Monopolistic Competition**
- Characteristics: Many firms, differentiated products, some barriers to entry
- Firm differentiation: Location, brand, quality, service create product uniqueness
- Pricing power: Downward-sloping demand curve; P > MC in equilibrium
- Economic rents: Stronger differentiation → higher rents; weaker substitutes → more pricing power
- Long-run: Entry of new competitors erodes rents; equilibrium at zero economic profit
- Limits to differentiation: Customers may not value differences, competitors imitate, costs may exceed premium

**Oligopoly**
- Characteristics: Few firms, similar or differentiated products, high barriers to entry
- Strategic interdependence: Each firm's profits depend on competitors' actions
- Pricing models:
  - Cournot (quantity competition): Firms choose quantity simultaneously; price determined by demand
  - Bertrand (price competition): Firms choose price simultaneously; with identical products, price = marginal cost
  - Stackelberg (sequential): Leader chooses first; follower responds; leader gets first-mover advantage
- Collusion: Joint profit maximization as monopoly; unstable without enforcement mechanisms
- Cartel stability: Requires monitoring, punishment, barriers to entry, homogeneous products

**Monopoly**
- Characteristics: One seller, high barriers to entry, substantial pricing power
- Sources of monopoly power: Scale economies, switching costs, intellectual property, control of scarce resources, high entry barriers
- Pricing and output: Maximizes profit where MR = MC; charges P > MC; restricts output
- Deadweight loss: Lost surplus from units not produced that would create value
- Regulation: Natural monopolies typically regulated to prevent excessive pricing
- Innovation: Monopolists may under-invest (no competitive pressure) or over-invest (maintain dominance)
