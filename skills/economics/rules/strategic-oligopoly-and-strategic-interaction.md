**Cournot Competition (Quantity Leadership)**
- Setup: Each firm chooses quantity; price determined by market clearing
- Reaction function: Optimal quantity decreases as rival output increases
- Equilibrium: At intersection of reaction functions; both firms optimize given rival output
- Outcomes: P > MC (firms have market power); profit between monopoly and competition
- Convergence: As n → ∞, price → MC (perfect competition outcome with just quantity competition)

**Bertrand Competition (Price Leadership)**
- Setup: Firms simultaneously set prices; demand determined by price
- Paradox: With identical products, two firms sufficient to drive price to marginal cost
- Differentiation breaks paradox: Imperfect substitutes allow P > MC equilibrium
- Reality: Most markets differentiated; Bertrand with differentiation realistic
- Implication: Differentiation allows pricing power; worth premium investment

**Stackelberg Leadership (Sequential Interaction)**
- Setup: Leader chooses quantity first; follower observes and responds
- First-mover advantage: Leader produces more, earns higher profit than Cournot
- Follower disadvantage: Produces less, earns lower profit; must accommodate leader's choice
- Strategic implication: Moving first in market entry, price announcements creates advantage

**Collusion and Cartel Stability**
- Joint profit maximization: Divide market, charge monopoly price, share monopoly profit
- Instability: Individual firm tempted to cheat; undercutting captures entire market at near-monopoly price
- Prisoner's dilemma: Individually rational (cheat), collectively worse off (price war)
- Enforcement: Repeated games, transparent pricing, quick detection, credible punishment enable collusion
- Kinked demand curve: Explains sticky prices; firms believe price increases not matched, decreases matched
