**Central Bank Tools and Transmission**
- Open Market Operations: Buy/sell bonds to change money supply and interest rates
- Reserve Requirements: Change how much banks can lend; less commonly used today
- Discount Rate: Rate charged when banks borrow from central bank
- Quantitative Easing: When rates zero; buy long-term bonds to drive down long rates and inject liquidity

**Interest Rate Effects on Business**
- Cost of capital: Higher rates increase borrowing costs; discourage investment
- Asset valuations: Higher rates reduce present value of future cash flows; lower stock/real estate prices
- Consumer spending: Higher rates reduce purchasing power; lower demand
- Competitive dynamics: High-rate periods reduce entry; low rates increase competition

**Phillips Curve and Wage-Price Dynamics**
- Traditional: Inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment
- Breakdown: 1970s stagflation showed curve unstable
- Modern understanding: Forward-looking agents anticipate policy; Phillips Curve shifts with expected inflation
- Lucas critique: Policymakers can't exploit Phillips Curve; rational agents adjust expectations
- Practical: Tight labor markets eventually drive wage inflation; wage-price spiral requires credible central bank

**Inflation Impact on Business**
- Losers: Savers (money loses value), fixed-income earners, creditors
- Winners: Debtors (repay with devalued money), firms with pricing power
- Uncertainty: Menu costs (pricing changes expensive), planning difficult, capital allocation distorted
- Mitigation: Inflation-linked contracts, pricing power, hedging strategies
