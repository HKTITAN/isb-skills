**Externalities and Pigouvian Corrections**
- Negative externality: Cost to third parties; market quantity too high
- Positive externality: Benefit to third parties; market quantity too low
- Pigouvian tax: Tax = external marginal cost; internalizes externality
- Pigouvian subsidy: Subsidy = external marginal benefit
- Cap-and-trade: Government sets limit, allows trading; achieves same outcome with more flexibility

**Public Goods and Underprovision**
- Characteristics: Non-rival (use doesn't reduce availability), non-excludable (can't prevent use)
- Market failure: Free-rider problem; rational individuals don't pay; demand underrevealed
- Government provision: Funded through taxation; ensures adequate supply
- Examples: National defense, basic research, infrastructure, public health

**Natural Monopoly Regulation**
- Characteristics: One firm serves market more efficiently than multiple competitors
- Pricing: Unregulated monopoly charges high price, reduces quantity; creates deadweight loss
- Regulation: Rate-of-return regulation caps profit; universal service requirements ensure coverage
- Tradeoff: Regulation reduces profit motive to innovate; excessive regulation increases costs
