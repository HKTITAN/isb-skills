**Price Elasticity of Demand**
- Definition: Percentage change in quantity demanded / Percentage change in price
- Elastic (|Ed| > 1): Quantity changes more than price (percentage); revenue decreases when price increases
- Inelastic (|Ed| < 1): Quantity changes less than price; revenue increases when price increases
- Applications:
  - Luxury goods (elastic): Price increases reduce revenue (customers switch)
  - Necessities (inelastic): Price increases increase revenue (customers have few alternatives)
  - Pricing strategy: Monopolists charge higher markups in inelastic markets

**Income Elasticity**
- Definition: Percentage change in quantity demanded / Percentage change in income
- Normal goods (Ei > 0): Buy more as income rises (positive relationship)
  - Luxury goods (Ei > 1): Buy disproportionately more with income growth
  - Necessities (Ei < 1): Buy somewhat more with income growth
- Inferior goods (Ei < 0): Buy less as income rises (switch to better alternatives)
  - Example: Ramen noodles, used clothing

**Cross-Price Elasticity**
- Definition: Percentage change in quantity of good X / Percentage change in price of good Y
- Substitutes (Exy > 0): Good X bought more when Y becomes more expensive
- Complements (Exy < 0): Good X bought less when Y becomes more expensive
- Applications: Product bundling, pricing related products
