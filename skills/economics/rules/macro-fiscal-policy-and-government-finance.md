**Government Spending and Taxation**
- Automatic stabilizers: Unemployment benefits, welfare, taxes automatically adjust demand
  - Unemployment rises (recession) → Benefits paid → Demand maintained
  - Revenue falls with income → Effective tax cut supports spending
- Discretionary policy: Government consciously changes spending/taxes to influence economy
- Multiplier effects:
  - Spending multiplier: $1 government spending increases demand by $1 × multiplier (>1) because spending induces consumption spending
  - Tax multiplier: $1 tax cut increases demand by less than spending multiplier (effect is indirect)

**Crowding Out**
- Mechanism: Government spending increases interest rates; higher rates reduce private investment
- Complete crowding out: Government spending fully offset by reduced private investment; no net demand effect
- Partial crowding out: Private investment falls but less than government spending increase
- Conditions: More likely when economy at full employment (limited resources) and when sensitive to interest rates
- Evidence: Estimates suggest partial crowding out in most economies

**Ricardian Equivalence**
- Proposition: Timing of taxation doesn't matter; tax cut financed by deficit equivalent to tax increase
- Mechanism: Individuals recognize future taxes needed to pay off debt; save tax cuts to pay future taxes
- Requirements: Perfect capital markets, rational expectations, infinite horizons
- Empirical evidence: Mixed; suggests partial Ricardian equivalence; many respond to temporary tax cuts
- Limitations: Unrealistic assumptions; people don't perfectly anticipate future taxes

**Debt Sustainability**
- Debt dynamics: Primary deficit (spending - taxes) drives debt accumulation
- Debt-to-GDP ratio: Key metric; stable when growth rate > real interest rate
- Interest burden: High debt ratios raise interest burden; may become unsustainable
- Fiscal space: Limited ability to borrow limits policy flexibility
- Debt crises: When confidence lost, borrowing costs spike; can force austerity or default
