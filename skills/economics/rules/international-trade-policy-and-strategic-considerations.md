**Tariffs and Quotas**
- Tariff (import tax): Raises domestic price; protects producers; consumers lose more than producers gain
- Quota (import limit): Creates shortage; domestic price rises; less efficient than tariff (foreign exporters capture rents)
- Deadweight loss: Both create efficiency loss; quotas worse (rents go to foreign sellers, not government)
- Strategic: Protecting declining sectors delays adjustment; learning-by-doing in infant industries requires time limits

**Non-Tariff Barriers**
- Technical/sanitary standards: Appear non-discriminatory but exclude foreign competitors
- Local content requirements: Force foreign firms to use local inputs/labor
- Government procurement: Agencies prefer domestic (legal in some cases, WTO carve-outs)
- Regulatory complexity: Licensing delays, inspection requirements, procedures

**Strategic Trade Policy**
- Infant industry argument: Emerging industries need protection until competitive
- Reality: Many never mature; protection becomes permanent; export-led growth outperforms protectionism
- Success: South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam with selective protection and time limits
- Failure: Latin America, India with permanent import substitution protection
