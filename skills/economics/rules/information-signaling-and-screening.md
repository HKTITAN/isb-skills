**Signaling: Informed Party Reveals Type**
- Definition: Informed party (seller, employee) takes costly action to credibly reveal quality/type
- Mechanism: Quality differences make signal costlier for low-quality to mimic
- Example education as signal: Smart workers' opportunity cost of time is lower (learn faster, earn more elsewhere)
  - Smart workers willing to get education when less-smart won't
  - Education costly for everyone, but differentially costly for low-ability workers
  - Education signals ability without teaching job skills
- Requirements: Signal costly, observable, differentially costly for bad types vs good types
- Business applications:
  - Warranties: High-quality producer willing to guarantee; low-quality won't
  - Money-back guarantees: Signal product confidence
  - Brand investment: Expensive advertising signals quality (wouldn't invest if product bad)
  - CEO commitment: Large insider shareholding signals confidence in firm

**Screening: Uninformed Party Extracts Information**
- Definition: Uninformed party (buyer, employer) designs contracts that induce revelation of hidden information
- Menu approach: Offer different contracts appealing to different types
- Example employment: Offer high-salary/low-job-security vs. moderate-salary/high-security
  - Skilled workers self-select into high-salary job (confident in ability)
  - Unskilled workers self-select into secure job (risk-averse)
  - Contracts' terms reveal worker type

**Screening Mechanisms**
- Diagnostic testing: Aptitude tests, interviews reveal ability
- Trial periods: Probation reveals actual performance
- Quantity discounts: Separate small buyers from large (reveal customer type)
- Adverse selection contracts: Higher-deductible insurance attracts high-risk (willing to bear risk)
- Design requirement: Make it individually optimal for each type to choose contracts revealing true type; mimicking must be costlier than honesty

**Separating vs. Pooling Equilibria**
- Separating equilibrium: Different types choose different contracts, revealing information
  - Requires screening/signaling to be credible
  - Prices reflect individual risk/quality
- Pooling equilibrium: All types choose same contract, information unrevealed
  - Occurs when signaling/screening too costly
  - Prices reflect average quality
  - Efficiency loss: Good types underpriced, bad types overpriced
- Equilibrium depends on information cost relative to benefit of revelation
