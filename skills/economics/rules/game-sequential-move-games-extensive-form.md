**Subgame Perfect Equilibrium**
- Definition: Equilibrium where strategies are optimal not just overall but at every decision node
- Backward induction: Start at end nodes, work backward to find optimal plays
- Eliminates incredible threats: Threats that player wouldn't actually carry out in equilibrium
- Example: Stackelberg model where leader moves first, follower responds

**First-Mover Advantage (Stackelberg)**
- Leader moves first; follower observes and responds
- Leader anticipates follower's reaction; produces more, sets favorable terms
- Outcome: Leader earns higher profit than in simultaneous-move (Cournot) equilibrium
- Follower worse off than in Cournot
- Conditions: Requires commitment ability; if leader can be ignored, first-mover advantage disappears

**Threat and Promise Credibility**
- Binding commitment: Public announcement, sunk costs (destroying alternative), contractual obligations
- Examples: CEO publicly commits to price stability (reputation at stake); franchise commitments (store built in market)
- Cheap talk: Unsubstantiated claims not credible; threats must be costly to carry out or enforced by structure
- Reputation building: Repeated interactions create credibility as player earns reputation for following through
