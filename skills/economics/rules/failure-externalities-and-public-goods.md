**Externalities**
- Definition: Cost/benefit borne by third party not compensated
- Negative externalities: Cost to third parties (pollution, noise)
- Positive externalities: Benefit to third parties (education, vaccines)
- Market failure: Market quantity differs from socially optimal quantity
  - Negative externality: Market quantity too high (private benefit > social benefit)
  - Positive externality: Market quantity too low (private benefit < social benefit)

**Pigouvian Taxes and Subsidies**
- Pigouvian tax: Tax equal to external marginal cost; internalizes externality
- Effect: Raises cost of production; reduces quantity to socially optimal level
- Pollution tax: Tax per unit of pollution equal to marginal environmental damage
- Subsidy: Negative Pigouvian tax for positive externality; lowers cost; increases quantity
- Implementation: Requires estimating external cost; politically difficult

**Cap-and-Trade**
- Mechanism: Government limits total pollution; firms can trade permits
- Efficiency: Achieves same outcome as Pigouvian tax; firms with low abatement costs reduce first
- Advantages: Certainty on quantity; flexibility on price
- Disadvantages: Political resistance; permit allocation affects distribution

**Public Goods**
- Definition: Non-rival (use by one doesn't reduce availability), non-excludable (can't prevent use)
- Underprovision: Market provides less than socially optimal; free-rider problem
  - Free-riders benefit without paying (can't exclude)
  - Rational individuals don't pay; demand underrevealed
- Government provision: Funded through taxation
- Examples: National defense, basic research, infrastructure

**Common Resources**
- Definition: Rivalrous (use reduces availability) but non-excludable (can't prevent use)
- Overuse: Tragedy of commons; individuals use beyond socially optimal because don't bear full cost
- Solutions: Privatization (create excludability), regulation (limit use), community management (social norms)
