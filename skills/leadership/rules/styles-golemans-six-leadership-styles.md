Daniel Goleman identified six distinct leadership styles, each creating different emotional climates and outcomes:

**1. Directive** (Do as I say)
- Approach: Clear instructions, immediate compliance, close monitoring
- Effectiveness: High for crises, inexperienced teams, routine tasks
- Emotional impact: Can feel controlling; stifles initiative if overused
- Risk: Creates dependency; reduces intrinsic motivation

**2. Visionary** (Come with me)
- Approach: Paint compelling picture of future; inspire toward bold goals
- Effectiveness: High for change, when direction is unclear, motivating teams
- Emotional impact: Energizing; creates shared purpose
- Risk: Can seem grandiose; may lack execution detail

**3. Affiliative** (People come first)
- Approach: Build harmony, strengthen relationships, create positive climate
- Effectiveness: High for building trust, during stress, team cohesion
- Emotional impact: Supportive; people feel valued
- Risk: May avoid necessary conflict; performance feedback delayed

**4. Participative** (What do you think?)
- Approach: Involve others in decisions, build consensus, leverage wisdom
- Effectiveness: High for gaining buy-in, accessing team knowledge, complex problems
- Emotional impact: Empowering; people feel heard
- Risk: Slow decision-making; works poorly in crisis

**5. Pacesetting** (Do as I do, now)
- Approach: Set high standards, work intensely, demand excellence
- Effectiveness: High for highly skilled teams, urgency
- Emotional impact: Can feel pressured; creates stress
- Risk: Burnout; low motivation; people feel watched

**6. Coaching** (I want to develop you)
- Approach: Help people develop, delegate, build capability
- Effectiveness: High for developing capability, long-term engagement
- Emotional impact: Supportive; people feel invested in
- Risk: Time-intensive; requires patience

**Most effective leaders flex between styles** based on situation and person. Poor leaders have one dominant style and overuse it.
