**Coordination mechanisms** - how teams align without chaos:

1. **Hierarchy**: Clear authority, chain of command; efficient for routine work, slow for innovation
2. **Shared goals**: Alignment on outcomes; enables autonomy while maintaining direction
3. **Standards and processes**: Documented methods ensure consistency and reduce coordination overhead
4. **Lateral communication**: Cross-functional connections, shared understanding, reduced silos
5. **Communities of practice**: Informal networks of people with shared expertise; knowledge sharing and capability building

**Tuckman's Team Development Model**:

- **Forming**: Team assembled, goals unclear, relationships polite; leader provides direction and structure
- **Storming**: Conflicts emerge, power struggles, resistance to leader; leader coaches through productive conflict
- **Norming**: Shared norms establish, cohesion increases, interdependence grows; leader enables peer accountability
- **Performing**: Team functions autonomously, flexible, focused on goals; leader removes obstacles, develops capability

Not all teams reach performing; regression occurs under stress. Effective leaders recognize which stage and adjust support.

**Psychological safety** - team members trust they can speak up, admit mistakes, ask questions without punishment or humiliation. Enables:
- Error detection and correction
- Knowledge sharing and learning
- Innovation and risk-taking
- Speaking uncomfortable truths

Built through: Leader vulnerability, rewarding questions, redirecting not punishing mistakes, making it safe to dissent.

**Diversity in teams**: Cognitive diversity (different ways of thinking) drives innovation and error-catching. But demographic diversity only creates these benefits if psychological safety exists; otherwise it increases conflict and reduces performance. Leaders must actively build inclusion.

**Managing star performers**: High performers often expect autonomy, visibility, rapid advancement. Risks: Burnout, entitlement, team resentment, departure if not carefully managed. Strategies: Clear career paths, sponsorship, stretching assignments, peer accountability, protecting from overload.

**Star performer management strategies**:
- Recognize publicly; give credit visibly
- Provide continuous stretch (boredom drives departure)
- Sponsor for visibility and advancement
- Ensure compensation is competitive (often underpaid relative to market)
- Involve in strategic initiatives
- Create peer accountability (stars can dominate team if unchecked)
- Protect from overload without limiting opportunity
- Don't allow resentment from others to go unaddressed

**Diversity and inclusion in teams**: Cognitive diversity (different backgrounds, perspectives, thinking styles) drives innovation and error-catching. Demographic diversity only realizes these benefits if:
- Psychological safety exists (people comfortable expressing different views)
- Team processes invite diverse input
- Leaders actively solicit minority perspectives
- Organizational culture values diversity

Without these conditions, demographic diversity can increase conflict without improving outcomes. Leaders must actively build inclusion.

**Incentive systems and unintended consequences**: What you measure and reward is what you get—often with unforeseen side effects:
- Individual incentives reduce cooperation; team incentives encourage collaboration
- Piece-rate pay increases quantity but reduces quality; can incentivize shortcuts
- Focusing on cost reduction alone can degrade customer experience or safety
- Rewarding activity (hours worked) instead of outcomes reduces autonomy
- Short-term incentives can sacrifice long-term value
- Design incentives considering full system effects

**Addressing incentive problems**:
1. Make unintended consequences visible (measurement)
2. Adjust incentive design to balance multiple outcomes
3. Combine incentives with cultural values (not just monetary rewards)
4. Include feedback mechanisms so people see impact
5. Revisit regularly as conditions change
