**Hofstede's 5 Cultural Dimensions** (describe national cultures):

1. **Power distance**: How unequal power distribution is accepted
   - Low: Flatter hierarchies, challenge authority, direct communication
   - High: Respect for hierarchy, obedience, indirect communication when disagreeing

2. **Individualism-Collectivism**: Personal vs. group identity
   - High individualism: Individual achievement, self-reliance, personal loyalty
   - High collectivism: Group harmony, interdependence, in-group loyalty

3. **Uncertainty avoidance**: Comfort with ambiguity and risk
   - Low: Comfortable with change, risk-taking, flexibility
   - High: Seek stability, rules, clear expectations, risk-averse

4. **Masculinity-Femininity**: Achievement and competition vs. relationships and quality of life
   - Masculine: Success, competition, material reward
   - Feminine: Cooperation, relationships, community well-being

5. **Long-term vs. Short-term orientation**: Future planning vs. immediate gratification
   - Long-term: Patience, savings, persistence, adapt to circumstances
   - Short-term: Respect for tradition, quick results, stability

**GLOBE Study** (Global Leadership and Organizational Behavior Effectiveness): Expands Hofstede research across 61 countries.

Key findings:
- Organizational culture can be changed through leadership and systems, unlike national culture which is more fixed
- Leadership effectiveness is both universal and culturally contingent (some behaviors universally effective; others context-dependent)
- Performance orientation (emphasis on results) and humane orientation (people-focused) are universal values
- Some effective leader behaviors vary: Charisma expected in some cultures but not others; decision style preferences vary

**Implications for global leaders**:
- Understand both national culture of country and organizational culture
- Adapt your leadership approach to cultural context
- Build trust across cultures through consistency and genuine interest

**Cultural Intelligence (CQ)** - ability to function effectively across cultures. Stronger predictor of global leadership success than IQ or technical expertise:

**1. Cognitive CQ**: Knowledge of cultural differences and similarities; frameworks for understanding cultures
- Knowledge of Hofstede dimensions, cultural economics, business practices
- Understanding how cultures differ in communication styles, decision-making, relationships

**2. Metacognitive CQ**: Awareness of cultural assumptions; ability to check and adjust your own thinking
- Recognizing your own cultural lens (not everyone thinks like you)
- Questioning your assumptions before acting
- Flexibility to revise mental models based on evidence

**3. Motivational CQ**: Intrinsic interest in learning about different cultures; confidence in cross-cultural interactions
- Genuine curiosity rather than stereotyping
- Confidence in ability to navigate cultural differences
- Willingness to invest effort in cross-cultural relationships

**4. Behavioral CQ**: Ability to adopt appropriate behaviors, communication patterns, and interaction styles
- Speaking people's communication preferences
- Using nonverbal communication appropriately
- Building relationships across cultural boundaries
- Flexibility in approach based on cultural context

**Developing CQ**:
- Deep immersion in different cultures (not just tourism)
- Relationships with cultural mentors who can explain unwritten rules
- Studying specific cultures you work with regularly
- Self-reflection on your own cultural assumptions
- Practicing new behaviors in safe settings before applying broadly

**Managing global teams**: Multiple time zones, languages, communication norms, values differences.

Effective strategies:
- Build relationships with individuals beyond transactions
- Establish clear norms and decision processes (written, explicit)
- Use synchronous communication (video calls) for important discussions requiring nuance
- Document decisions carefully; written context compensates for lack of face-to-face
- Create opportunities for informal connection (virtual coffee, social channels)
- Be sensitive to time zone impacts; rotate meeting times
- Clarify communication expectations (response time, formality level)
- Acknowledge and leverage diverse perspectives as strength

**Expatriate management**: International assignments have high failure rate (30-40% complete early due to family issues, cultural adjustment, career uncertainty).

Support strategies:
- **Pre-departure**: Cultural training covering history, economics, social norms, business practices
- **Selection**: Assess cultural adaptability, family stability, language ability, motivation
- **During assignment**: Mentoring from experienced expatriates, sponsor support from headquarters
- **Spouse/family support**: Language training, job assistance for spouse, social networks
- **Clear career path**: Define what happens after assignment (advancement, return timeline, reputation)
- **Return integration**: Repatriation shock is real; people struggle re-adjusting to headquarters culture
- **Leverage learning**: Use expatriate experience for organizational learning, not just for filling position
