## title: "Cialdini's Six Principles of Influence"
impact: MEDIUM
impactDescription: "Supporting concept that enhances domain understanding"
tags: cialdini, influence, leadership, principles, six

**Reciprocity**: People feel obligated to return favors and treat others as they've been treated.

- Application: Give value first; do favors without immediate expectation of return; create norm of exchange
- Risk: Can be manipulative if fake generosity leads to guilt-based obligation

**Commitment and Consistency**: People want to be consistent with their stated positions and commitments.

- Application: Get small commitments first (foot-in-the-door); make commitments public to strengthen them
- Risk: Can lock people into positions even when circumstances change; resistance to new information

**Social proof**: People look to others' behavior to determine their own.

- Application: Show what similar others are doing; highlight testimonials and social norms; make peer behavior visible
- Risk: Can amplify harmful behaviors (mob behavior) or suppress minority views

**Authority**: People respect expertise and tend to comply with credible experts.

- Application: Build your expertise visibly; leverage expert testimonials; cite credentials
- Risk: Blind obedience to authority; "expert" can be falsely claimed

**Liking**: People prefer to say yes to those they like.

- Application: Build genuine liking through similarity, compliments, cooperation; invest in relationships
- Risk: Can override objective judgment; creates bias toward likeable people

**Scarcity**: People value things more when they're rare or becoming less available.

- Application: Highlight time limits, limited quantities, exclusive opportunities; genuine scarcity works better than artificial
- Risk: Artificial scarcity creates distrust when discovered

**Strategic application**: Ethical persuasion combines these principles honestly and transparently. Manipulation uses scarcity and authority to short-circuit rational thinking.
