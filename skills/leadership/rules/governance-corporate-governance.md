**The governance structure** defines how organizations are governed—who has authority, how decisions get made, and how accountability is established.

**Board of Directors**: Represents shareholders; oversees CEO; sets strategic direction.

**Key board responsibilities**:
- Approve strategy and major decisions
- Oversee financial management
- Ensure compliance with law and ethics
- Manage CEO and executive compensation
- Plan for CEO succession
- Monitor risk

**CEO-Board Relationship**:
- Trust-based: CEO transparent, board supportive but challenging
- Adversarial: CEO manages board information, board distrusts CEO
- Effective governance requires trust and healthy skepticism

**Board Committees**:
- **Audit**: Oversees financial reporting, compliance, internal controls
- **Compensation**: Sets executive pay, ensures alignment
- **Governance**: Ensures board quality, ethics, succession planning
- **Risk**: Identifies and manages enterprise risk

**Board Dynamics**:
- Board quality depends on director diversity (backgrounds, thinking styles), independence, engagement
- Insider boards (many current/former executives) are less effective at oversight
- Activist boards (pushing specific agendas) can create conflict
- Passive boards (rubber-stamping) fail to oversee
