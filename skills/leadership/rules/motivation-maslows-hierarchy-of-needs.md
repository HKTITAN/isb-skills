Maslow proposes that humans are motivated by a hierarchy of needs, progressing from basic to higher-order:

**The Five Levels:**

1. **Physiological needs** (food, shelter, sleep): Without these, nothing else matters
2. **Safety needs** (security, stability, predictability): Job security, clear expectations
3. **Belonging/Love needs** (connection, community, relationships): Team membership, social connection
4. **Esteem needs** (respect, recognition, achievement): Status, recognition, autonomy
5. **Self-actualization** (fulfilling potential, personal growth): Mastery, purpose, contribution

**Leadership application**: As lower needs are met, higher needs become motivators. Offering more salary (physiological) won't motivate someone who already feels secure but lacks belonging. Conversely, recognition won't satisfy someone worried about job loss.

**Limitations**: Hierarchy is not always linear. Purpose can override safety (people take risks for meaningful work). Cultural differences affect which needs are prioritized.
