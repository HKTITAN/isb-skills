Five distinct approaches to handling conflict; each appropriate in different contexts:

**1. Competing** ("Win-lose")
- Assertive, uncooperative; pursue your interests at expense of others
- When to use: Crisis requiring quick decision, important principle at stake, someone is taking advantage of you, other party won't cooperate
- Downsides: Damages relationships, creates resentment, suppresses valuable input from others

**2. Collaborating** ("Win-win")
- Assertive and cooperative; seek solutions that satisfy everyone
- When to use: Both parties have important interests, integration creates superior solution, relationship is important and ongoing, time permits exploring options
- Downsides: Time-consuming, not all situations have win-win solutions, can be seen as weak if concessions are misread

**3. Compromising** ("Split the difference")
- Moderate assertiveness and cooperation; each party gives something up
- When to use: Parties have equal power and importance, time pressure exists, win-win isn't possible, need to move forward despite impasse
- Downsides: Both parties slightly dissatisfied, can become standard approach even when better solutions exist

**4. Avoiding** ("Ignore it")
- Unassertive and uncooperative; withdraw from conflict
- When to use: Issue is trivial, more important issues need energy, relationship would be damaged by addressing it, need to buy time, other party needs to cool down
- Downsides: Problems fester, resentment builds, important issues don't get addressed, can be perceived as indifference

**5. Accommodating** ("Lose-win")
- Cooperative but unassertive; prioritize other party's interests
- When to use: Issue is more important to other party than you, maintaining relationship is critical, you realize you're wrong, other party has legitimate need
- Downsides: Your interests get sacrificed, can create imbalance in relationship if overused, can be seen as weakness

**Strategic flexibility**: Effective leaders vary approach based on situation. Problem: Most people have one dominant mode and overuse it.
