**Negotiation styles** (beyond just tactics):

**Distributive (win-lose) negotiation**: Fixed pie; one party's gain is other's loss
- Appropriate when: One-time transaction, clear opposition of interests, limited relationship importance
- Approach: Anchoring, making small concessions, knowing your BATNA
- Risk: Creates resentment; potential for ongoing relationship damage

**Integrative (win-win) negotiation**: Expandable pie; find trades that benefit both
- Appropriate when: Ongoing relationship, multiple issues of different importance, creative solutions possible
- Approach: Understand underlying interests, identify differences in priorities, bundle issues
- Risk: Time-consuming; assumes shared interests exist

**Most business negotiations have both elements**: Some zero-sum issues (price) and some where both can benefit (terms, timing, future business).

**Power in negotiation** comes from:
- **BATNAs** (yours and theirs): Better alternatives make better negotiators
- **Information**: Knowing their constraints, deadlines, priorities
- **Time**: Patience; ability to walk away; not desperate
- **Standards**: Market rates, precedent, objective criteria
- **Relationships**: Trust reduces need for aggressive tactics

**Negotiation preparation checklist**:
- What's my BATNA? What's my reservation price?
- What's theirs? (Research, inference)
- What issues matter most to each side?
- What trades can I offer?
- What's my opening offer? (High but defensible)
- What will I concede on? In what order?
- What's non-negotiable?
- What are their constraints? (Timing, politics, budget)

**Common negotiation mistakes**:
- **Poor preparation**: Unprepared negotiators make bad deals
- **Revealing your BATNA**: Weakens your position
- **Early concessions**: Sets tone; creates expectation of continued movement
- **Ignoring interests**: Negotiating positions instead of interests
- **Taking disagreement personally**: Makes finding solutions harder
- **Not exploring trades**: Missing integrative possibilities
- **Accepting first offer**: Often negotiable if you ask
