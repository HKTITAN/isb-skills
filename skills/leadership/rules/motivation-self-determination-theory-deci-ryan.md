Self-Determination Theory proposes that intrinsic motivation comes from three psychological needs:

**Autonomy**: Sense of choice and control over your work.
- People perform better when they choose how to do work, not just what to do
- Micromanagement undermines autonomy and intrinsic motivation
- Leaders: Provide direction on outcomes, not methods

**Competence**: Feeling effective and capable.
- People want to master their work and improve
- Repeated failure without support erodes motivation
- Leaders: Provide feedback, coaching, stretch assignments that develop capability

**Relatedness**: Connection to others and sense of belonging.
- People care more about their work when connected to others
- Isolation undermines motivation even if autonomy and competence are high
- Leaders: Build team connection; help people see how their work matters to others

**Key insight**: The most motivated people have intrinsic motivation (autonomy + competence + relatedness), not external rewards. Excessive monitoring, unclear feedback, or team isolation kills intrinsic motivation no matter how much you pay.
