**Why ethics matter**:
- Legal requirements: Regulatory compliance is minimum
- Stakeholder trust: Ethics violations destroy trust and relationships
- Long-term value: Companies that cut ethical corners fail when caught
- Culture: Ethical standards set from the top; violations from leaders poison culture
- Personal integrity: Leading unethically creates cognitive dissonance and stress

**Ethical frameworks for decision-making**:

**Consequentialist approach** (utilitarian ethics): Judge decisions by outcomes—maximize good for greatest number
- Core question: What will produce the best overall consequences?
- Strength: Focuses on impact and outcomes; pragmatic
- Weakness: Hard to predict all consequences; unintended effects; can justify harmful short-term actions for long-term good; requires calculating "good" which is subjective
- Leadership application: Useful for balancing trade-offs; focus on outcomes matters in business
- Risk: Can justify unethical actions ("end justifies means")

**Deontological approach** (duty-based ethics): Judge decisions by adherence to principles—some things are right/wrong regardless of outcome
- Core question: What are my duties and obligations? What's the right thing to do?
- Strength: Provides clear principles; doesn't compromise values for results; protects vulnerable
- Weakness: Principles can conflict; rigid application ignores context; can lead to outcomes that harm
- Leadership application: Creates ethical guardrails; protects from rationalization
- Risk: Inflexibility; adherence to principle even when consequences are clearly harmful

**Virtue ethics approach**: Judge decisions by whether they reflect good character—what would a virtuous person do?
- Core question: What character traits do I want to develop? What would a person of integrity do?
- Strength: Focuses on character development and relationships; acknowledges complexity
- Weakness: Virtues can conflict; subjective; doesn't provide clear decision rules
- Leadership application: Guides character development; considers relationship implications
- Risk: Vague; can rationalize different "virtues"

**Care ethics approach**: Judge decisions by considering relationships and interdependence; prioritize meeting needs of those affected
- Core question: Who is affected? What do they need? How can I respond with care and sensitivity?
- Strength: Emphasizes relationships and caring; highlights vulnerability
- Weakness: Can be paternalistic; hard to scale; can create favoritism
- Leadership application: Balances individual needs with organizational goals
- Risk: Preferential treatment; lack of fairness across people

**Rights-based approach**: Judge decisions by respect for individual rights and freedoms
- Core question: What are people's fundamental rights? Does this decision violate them?
- Strength: Protects individuals; clear principles
- Weakness: Rights can conflict; doesn't address collective good
- Leadership application: Protects employee rights; sets boundaries on what's acceptable
- Risk: Can prioritize individual rights over organizational needs

**Practical approach for leaders**: Use all frameworks. Ask:
1. **Virtue**: What character traits do I want to develop through this decision? What would integrity look like?
2. **Duty**: What are my obligations? To whom? What duties conflict?
3. **Consequences**: What outcomes will this decision produce? For whom?
4. **Care/Relationships**: How will this affect relationships? Who is most vulnerable?
5. **Rights**: Whose rights are affected? Are any violated?

Different frameworks often point in same direction for truly ethical decisions. When they conflict, the decision is genuinely difficult. Acknowledge the trade-offs rather than pretending there's an easy answer.

**Managing ethical dilemmas**:
1. Recognize the dilemma—don't rationalize it away
2. Gather facts—understand what's really happening
3. Consider all stakeholders—whose interests are affected?
4. Apply frameworks—what do different ethical approaches suggest?
5. Decide—make a choice aligned with your values
6. Communicate—explain your reasoning transparently
7. Follow through—live with your decision consistently

**Building ethical culture**:
- **Top commitment**: CEO and board must visibly prioritize ethics
- **Clear standards**: Make values and expectations explicit
- **Safe reporting**: Enable people to report ethical concerns without fear
- **Accountability**: Hold people responsible for ethics, not just results
- **Consequences**: Address ethical violations promptly and visibly
- **Modeling**: Leaders must demonstrate ethical behavior
