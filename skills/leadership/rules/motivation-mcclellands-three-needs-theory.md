McClelland proposes three primary motivational drives that vary by person:

**Achievement Need** (nAch): Drive to accomplish, excel, and reach challenging goals.
- Motivated by: Clear goals, feedback, moderate challenge, personal responsibility
- Risks: Can focus on personal achievement over team; may over-commit
- Leadership approach: Give achievers stretching goals, clear metrics, autonomy

**Affiliation Need** (nAff): Drive for connection, belonging, and harmonious relationships.
- Motivated by: Team collaboration, social connection, harmony, cooperation
- Risks: May avoid necessary conflict; can be risk-averse to protect relationships
- Leadership approach: Foster team bonding; involve in group decisions; emphasize collaboration

**Power Need** (nPow): Drive to influence, lead, and have impact.
- Motivated by: Leadership opportunities, influence, status, autonomy over others
- Risks: Can become domineering; may pursue power over group welfare
- Leadership approach: Create leadership opportunities; involve in decisions; provide visibility

**Application**: Diagnose which drives are strongest in each team member. Achievement-driven people need challenging goals; affiliation-driven need team cohesion; power-driven need leadership roles. Same reward won't motivate everyone.
