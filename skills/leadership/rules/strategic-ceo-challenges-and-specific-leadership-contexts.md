**Being the CEO is fundamentally different** from being a leader below:
- No boss to provide guidance; limited trusted confidantes
- Pressure to have answers; limited admissions of uncertainty
- Visibility high; every decision watched
- Loneliness; can't build peer relationships with direct reports
- Termination risk; board can remove you

**CEO priorities** (in order):
1. **Strategy**: Ensure organization is pursuing right market, product, positioning
2. **Capital allocation**: Money follows strategy; where resources go determines company direction
3. **Culture and talent**: Culture determines how strategy gets executed; need right people
4. **Stakeholder management**: Board, investors, employees, customers, regulators all need attention
5. **Personal effectiveness**: Your energy, focus, and judgment cascade throughout organization

**CEO decision-making categories** (see earlier section):
- Directional decisions (strategy, M&A, business model)
- Operational decisions (delegate to functional leaders)
- Crisis decisions (personal involvement required)
- Cultural decisions (set norms, values, hiring standards)

**Common CEO Failures**:
- Micromanaging: Doesn't delegate; suffocates organization
- Lack of focus: Pursuing too many priorities simultaneously
- Board relationship failures: Not transparent, dismissive, or overly dependent
- Underestimating execution: Great strategy poorly executed fails
- Ignoring culture: Culture misalignment kills otherwise good strategies
- Weak management team: Not developing depth, not replacing underperformers
- Losing perspective: Loses touch with customers, market reality

**CEO relationship with board**:
- Board is your boss; relationship is critical
- Regular communication essential; no surprises
- Transparency about challenges builds trust
- Board provides governance, oversight, counsel
- Board has authority to remove CEO
- Best boards have mix of challenge and support

**CEO security**: Feeling secure enough to take intelligent risks while staying grounded to reality.
- Know your BATNA (what would you do if fired?)
- Build relationships outside the company
- Maintain perspective through outside directors, mentors, coaches
- Remember you're steward of the organization, not owner
