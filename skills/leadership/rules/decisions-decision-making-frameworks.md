**Prospect Theory** (explains actual vs. rational choice):

- **Loss aversion**: Losses feel about twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good
  - Impact: Risk-averse in potential gain situations (hold winners too long), risk-seeking when facing losses (double down on bets)

- **Reference dependence**: Decisions measured against reference point, not absolute outcomes
  - Impact: Reframing reference point changes decisions (glass half full vs. half empty)

Implication: Frame decisions carefully; recognize when emotions distort choices; design systems that counteract predictable biases.

**System 1 vs. System 2 thinking**:

- **System 1**: Fast, automatic, emotional, effortless thinking (gut feel, intuition)
- **System 2**: Slow, deliberate, analytical, effortful thinking (careful reasoning)

Leaders rely too much on System 1 for complex decisions. Strategy: Use System 1 for intuitive judgments in your expertise; use System 2 for important decisions outside your expertise, decisions with irreversible consequences, or high-stakes situations. Recognize when System 1 is hijacking System 2.

**Bounded rationality**: Humans can't process unlimited information; we satisfice (find "good enough") rather than maximize (find absolute best).

Implication: You're never working with perfect information; make good decisions with available data, build in optionality, learn and adapt.

**Cynefin Framework** (decision-making by domain):

- **Clear domain**: Cause-effect relationships are obvious. Process: Sense-Categorize-Respond. Use best practices.
- **Complicated domain**: Cause-effect requires analysis. Process: Sense-Analyze-Respond. Use expert knowledge.
- **Complex domain**: Cause-effect only clear in hindsight. Process: Probe-Sense-Respond. Experiment, learn, adapt.
- **Chaotic domain**: No time for analysis. Process: Act-Sense-Respond. Stabilize first, then move to complex.
- **Disorder**: Unknown which domain you're in. Clarify before deciding.

Leaders often apply Clear domain solutions to Complex problems, which fails. Conversely, they over-analyze Clear problems.

**OODA Loop** (John Boyd - decision-making under uncertainty):

- **Observe**: Gather current information
- **Orient**: Filter through experience, culture, genetics, training
- **Decide**: Choose course of action
- **Act**: Execute decision

Speed matters. The side that goes through OODA faster adapts faster. But Orientation is the most important phase—speed is worthless if your mental model is wrong. Effective decision-making requires regularly challenging your orientation (assumptions, mental models).

**Vroom-Yetton Decision Model** (when to involve others):

- **Autocratic (A1)**: You decide alone with available information
- **Consultative (C1)**: You gather info from others, then decide alone
- **Consultative (C2)**: You involve others in decision-making together
- **Consensual (G2)**: Group decides by consensus; you facilitate

Choose based on: Decision quality requirements, acceptability requirements (will people accept the decision?), time available, and likelihood that involving others improves quality or acceptance.
