Effective performance management translates motivation theory into systems:

**Goal Setting** (MBO - Management by Objectives):
- Specific, measurable goals are more motivating than vague expectations
- People perform better when they participate in goal-setting
- Regular feedback beats annual reviews
- Cascading goals align individual work to organizational priorities

**Incentive Design**:
- Individual incentives can reduce cooperation; team incentives encourage collaboration
- Piece-rate pay increases quantity but often reduces quality
- Results-only environments (focusing on outcomes, not hours) increase autonomy
- Recognize that what you measure is what you get—unintended consequences are common

**Feedback Systems**:
- Frequent, specific feedback is more motivating than annual ratings
- Positive feedback is underused; recognition has high leverage
- Critical feedback works better when delivered on a foundation of trust and positive regard
- Self-feedback (people rating themselves) should precede manager feedback to stimulate reflection

**Development and Growth**:
- People are more engaged when they see career paths
- Stretch assignments (challenging work with support) build capability and motivation
- Mentorship and coaching develop people more than training programs
- Visible advancement of developed people signals the organization invests in growth
