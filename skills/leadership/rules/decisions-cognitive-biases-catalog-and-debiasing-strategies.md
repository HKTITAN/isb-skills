**Anchoring bias**: Over-relying on first information encountered, which becomes reference point for all subsequent judgments.
- Example: First salary offer anchors entire negotiation
- Debiasing: Explicitly consider alternative anchors, delay accepting first number, seek multiple data points before deciding

**Availability bias**: Judging probability and frequency by how easily examples come to mind.
- Example: Recent plane crash makes flying seem dangerous; vivid customer complaint seems representative
- Debiasing: Gather base rate statistics, actively seek disconfirming examples, use systematic data over memorable anecdotes

**Confirmation bias**: Seeking, interpreting, and remembering information that confirms pre-existing beliefs.
- Example: Believing candidate is great, then overlooking negative signals
- Debiasing: Actively seek disconfirming evidence, assign someone to argue against your position (red teaming), delay forming initial opinions

**Overconfidence bias**: Overestimating the accuracy of your knowledge and predictions.
- Example: Forecasts prove wrong because confidence intervals were too narrow; underestimating project duration
- Debiasing: Track prediction accuracy over time, use reference class forecasting (how similar past projects actually performed), ask others for estimates before sharing yours

**Sunk cost fallacy**: Continuing commitment to failing projects because of past investment rather than future prospects.
- Example: Continuing bad acquisition because "we've already spent millions"; staying in bad marriage because "we've invested years"
- Debiasing: Explicitly separate past costs (irrelevant) from future costs/benefits; ask "would I make this decision today?" ignoring past investments

**Framing effects**: Same information presented differently (loss vs. gain) produces different decisions.
- Example: "90% success rate" vs. "10% failure rate" for same medical procedure produce different choices
- Debiasing: Present information in multiple frames, be aware that decisions may reflect framing rather than substance, use systematic processes rather than gut feel

**Hindsight bias**: Overestimating how predictable past events were ("I knew it all along").
- Example: Criticizing past leaders for "obvious" mistakes that seemed ambiguous at the time
- Debiasing: Document pre-decision expectations; study actual decision information available at time (not current information); use decision journals

**Status quo bias**: Preferring to maintain current state even when better alternatives exist.
- Example: Keeping ineffective processes because "that's how we've always done it"
- Debiasing: Periodically question default assumptions, mandate review of long-standing practices, highlight cost of inaction

**Dunning-Kruger effect**: People with low competence overestimate their knowledge and ability.
- Example: New manager overconfident in decision-making; learner thinks they understand topic after surface study
- Debiasing: Seek feedback from experts, build confidence in your actual capability gaps, reward intellectual humility
