## title: "Power, Politics, and Influence in Organizations"
impact: MEDIUM
impactDescription: "Supporting concept that enhances domain understanding"
tags: influence, organizations, politics, power, quantitative, technology

**Sources of organizational power**:

- **Legitimate**: Formal authority from position
- **Reward**: Control over resources, opportunities, recognition
- **Coercive**: Ability to punish or withhold benefits
- **Expert**: Knowledge and competence in valued domain
- **Referent**: Admiration and personal liking

Expert and referent power are most sustainable; coercive power creates resentment and resistance.

**Power and politics in organizations**: All organizations are political; recognizing this is necessary for effectiveness.

**Sources of organizational power**:

- **Legitimate power**: Formal authority from position; ability to direct others
- **Reward power**: Control over resources, opportunities, recognition, raises
- **Coercive power**: Ability to punish, withhold benefits, fire people
- **Expert power**: Knowledge and competence in valued domain; people defer to expertise
- **Referent power**: Admiration, liking, identification with person; people want to please them
- **Information power**: Access to critical information or connections

**Power dynamics**: Expert and referent power are most sustainable and effective. Coercive power creates resentment and resistance. Formal authority only works well when combined with expertise or referent power.

**Political skill**: Ability to understand organizational dynamics and influence others effectively. Stronger predictor of advancement than competence alone.

**Components of political skill**:

- **Social astuteness**: Reading people, understanding motivations, picking up on unspoken dynamics
- **Interpersonal influence**: Persuading others, adapting approach, creating shared understanding
- **Networking ability**: Building relationships, maintaining connections, accessing information and favors
- **Apparent sincerity**: Seeming authentic, trustworthy, not overly calculating

**High political skill enables getting things done** even with limited formal authority. It becomes problematic only when used manipulatively or selfishly (advancing self at expense of organization or others).

**Organizational politics**: Emerge from scarce resources, ambiguous goals, competing interests. Ways to navigate:

- Build strong relationships across the organization
- Understand informal power structures (who really influences decisions)
- Develop reputation for competence and trustworthiness
- Be transparent about your interests and values
- Don't take sides in turf wars unless necessary
- Build coalitions for important initiatives
- Recognize when conflict is about principle vs. ego

**Influence tactics** (in order of effectiveness):

**High effectiveness**:

- **Rational persuasion**: Logical argument with evidence; works especially well with analytical people
- **Inspirational appeals**: Appealing to values and vision; energizes people around shared purpose
- **Consultation**: Involving others in decision; builds buy-in and improves decision quality

**Medium effectiveness**:

- **Collaboration**: Offering help, partnership; builds relationships
- **Legitimating**: Citing rules, precedent, authority; provides justification
- **Personal appeals**: Appealing to personal relationship; works if relationship exists

**Lower effectiveness**:

- **Ingratiation**: Building liking through compliments, favors; works short-term but builds resentment if overdone
- **Exchange**: Trading benefits for support; transactional; doesn't build relationships
- **Pressure**: Demands, threats, urgent appeals; works when you have power; creates resentment

**Effective influencers** adapt tactics to audience and situation:

- Use rational persuasion with analytical audiences
- Use inspirational appeals when trying to drive change
- Use consultation to build buy-in on important decisions
- Avoid pressure except in crisis
- Avoid excessive ingratiation; builds distrust

**Ethical influence** uses any of these tactics honestly and with transparency about interests. Unethical influence manipulates or deceives. Manipulation (hidden agenda, false information) is self-defeating long-term; trust is foundation of influence.
