An integrated approach to understanding how three primary motivational drivers combine to create leadership effectiveness.

**Achievement motivation**: Drive to accomplish, excel, win
- High achievers: Set challenging goals, want feedback, take personal responsibility
- Leadership impact: Set high standards, push for results
- Risk: Overfocus on personal achievement; impatience with others

**Affiliation motivation**: Drive for connection, belonging, positive relationships
- High affiliation: Value teamwork, collaboration, harmony
- Leadership impact: Build team cohesion, create psychological safety
- Risk: Avoid necessary conflict; resist decisions that hurt relationships

**Power motivation**: Drive to influence, lead, have impact
- High power: Want to influence others, achieve goals through others
- Leadership impact: Mobilize people, create vision, build coalitions
- Risk: Can become domineering; focus on power over purpose

**Balanced leaders** have all three in moderate amounts:
- Sufficient achievement to drive results
- Sufficient affiliation to build relationships and team
- Sufficient power to influence and mobilize others

**Diagnosing imbalance**: Leaders high in achievement but low in affiliation can be ruthless. Leaders high in affiliation but low in achievement avoid accountability. Leaders high in power but low in affiliation become tyrants. Effective development involves strengthening weaker dimensions.
