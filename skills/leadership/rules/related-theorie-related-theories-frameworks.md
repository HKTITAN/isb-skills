**Expectancy Theory (Vroom)**: People are motivated when they believe effort leads to performance (expectancy), performance leads to outcomes (instrumentality), and outcomes are valued (valence).

**Equity Theory (Adams)**: People compare their effort-to-reward ratio with others; perceived inequity causes dissatisfaction.

**Goal Setting Theory (Locke & Latham)**: Specific, difficult goals increase motivation and performance more than vague or easy goals; feedback and commitment matter.

**Situational Leadership (Hersey & Blanchard)**: Leader's style should match team member's capability and commitment. As people develop, leaders should reduce direction and increase support.

**Transformational Leadership (Burns)**: Leaders transform followers' values and aspirations, moving them beyond self-interest to organizational interest.

**Path-Goal Theory (House)**: Leaders should clarify path to goals, remove obstacles, and provide support. Leader style should match task clarity and team maturity.

**Attribution Theory**: People explain others' behavior based on internal factors (character, ability) or external factors (circumstance, luck). Leaders often attribute failures to internal factors for others but external factors for themselves.

**Implicit Leadership Theories**: People have mental models of what leaders should be like. Effectiveness partly depends on matching these implicit expectations.

**SBI Model for Feedback** (Situation-Behavior-Impact): Structure feedback by describing the situation, specific behavior observed, and impact it had. More effective than vague feedback like "You were rude."
