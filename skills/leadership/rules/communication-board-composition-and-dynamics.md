**Board Committees** (typical structure):
- **Audit**: Oversees financial reporting, compliance, internal controls, auditor independence
  - Meets regularly with external and internal audit
  - Reviews financial statements before approval
  
- **Compensation/People**: Sets executive pay, ensures alignment with performance, oversees succession planning and culture
  - Reviews compensation philosophy and competitiveness
  - Assesses alignment with risk tolerance
  - Oversees talent development and succession
  
- **Governance/Nominating**: Ensures board quality, ethics, succession planning
  - Identifies and recruits new board members
  - Assesses board effectiveness
  - Sets governance policies
  
- **Risk** (in larger/financial firms): Identifies and manages enterprise risk
  - Oversees risk management framework
  - Reviews major risk exposures
  - Assesses risk culture

**Board composition**:
- **Size**: Typically 5-15 directors; larger boards less effective
- **Independence**: Majority should be independent from management
- **Expertise**: Should have financial literacy, industry knowledge, functional expertise
- **Diversity**: Diversity of background, thinking style, industry experience improves oversight

**Board dynamics**:
- **Insider boards** (many current/former executives): Less effective at oversight; too close to CEO
- **Activist boards** (pushing specific agendas): Can create conflict and instability
- **Passive boards** (rubber-stamping): Fail to fulfill oversight role
- **Effective boards**: Challenge constructively, ask tough questions, engage in strategic dialogue with management

**CEO-Board relationship**:
- CEO reports to board; ultimate accountability
- Effective boards are neither adversarial nor passive
- CEO should prepare board for challenges before they become crises
- Board should provide counsel and guidance, not micromanage
- Regular executive sessions (board without management) normal practice

**Director expectations**:
- Attend meetings and prepare thoroughly
- Ask tough questions constructively
- Disclose conflicts of interest
- Maintain confidentiality
- Commit sufficient time
- Hold CEO accountable for performance
