Emotional intelligence—the ability to recognize, understand, and manage emotions in yourself and others—is a stronger predictor of leadership success than IQ.

**Five dimensions of EI**:

**1. Self-awareness**: Understanding your emotions, strengths, weaknesses, impact on others
- Leaders lack self-awareness when they're blindsided by feedback or don't understand why people react to them
- Development: 360-degree feedback, personal coaching, journaling on emotional triggers

**2. Self-regulation**: Managing your emotions; staying calm under pressure; controlling impulses
- High self-regulation: You stay composed when challenged, admit mistakes, keep long-term perspective
- Low self-regulation: You react defensively, blame others, make impulsive decisions when stressed
- Development: Meditation, recognizing triggers, creating pause between stimulus and response

**3. Motivation**: Drive to achieve; resilience in face of setbacks; intrinsic motivation
- Intrinsically motivated leaders inspire others; transactional leaders only get compliance
- Development: Connect work to purpose; celebrate progress; maintain optimism

**4. Empathy**: Understanding others' emotions, perspectives, needs
- High empathy: You listen deeply, consider others' viewpoints, adapt to different needs
- Low empathy: You assume people think/feel like you; dismiss different perspectives; miss nonverbal cues
- Development: Active listening; perspective-taking; asking questions

**5. Social skills**: Managing relationships, influence, building networks, collaboration
- Manifests as: Building trust, creating psychological safety, managing conflict, inspiring others
- Development: Practice communication skills, seek mentorship, expand network intentionally

**Developing EI**: Unlike IQ, EI can be developed throughout life. Development requires self-awareness (feedback), commitment to change, practice, and coaching.
