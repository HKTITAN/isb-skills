**Janis's Groupthink Model** - when cohesive groups make poor decisions:

**Preconditions**: Isolation, directive leader, high stress, lack of norms for reviewing assumptions.

**Eight symptoms indicating groupthink present**:

1. Illusion of invulnerability: "We can't fail"; excessive optimism
2. Collective rationalization: Dismissing warnings; explaining away negative feedback
3. Illusion of morality: Group believes its position is inherently moral
4. Stereotyping: Other side is evil/stupid; dismisses their arguments
5. Direct pressure on dissenters: "You're not a team player"
6. Self-censorship: Members suppress doubts to maintain harmony
7. Illusion of unanimity: Silence interpreted as agreement
8. Mindguards: Protecting group from contrary information

**Historical examples**:
- Bay of Pigs invasion: Advisors suppressed doubts about feasibility
- Space Shuttle Challenger: Engineers' concerns ignored by managers
- Enron: Executives and board ignored accounting irregularities; created culture where questioning was punished

**Prevention strategies**:

- Leader deliberately solicits dissent and plays devil's advocate
- Assign someone to argue against the group position (red team)
- Invite outside experts to challenge assumptions
- Break into subgroups to discuss separately before full group decides
- Encourage individuals to voice concerns without penalty
- Use decision processes that surface disagreement (structured debate, pre-mortems)
