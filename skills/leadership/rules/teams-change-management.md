**Kotter's 8-Step Change Model** (most comprehensive framework):

1. **Establish urgency**: Make compelling case for change; overcome complacency
2. **Build coalition**: Gather powerful sponsors and advocates; create leadership team for change
3. **Form vision and strategy**: Paint picture of future state; develop pathway to get there
4. **Communicate vision**: Repeat message through multiple channels; make it credible and compelling
5. **Empower others**: Remove obstacles; create quick wins; celebrate progress
6. **Generate short-term wins**: Show early results; build momentum; maintain morale
7. **Consolidate gains**: Prevent regression; institutionalize changes; tackle remaining issues
8. **Anchor in culture**: Make changes permanent; embed in systems, processes, and leadership

Failures occur when skipping steps (often urgency or wins) or when leaders declare victory too early.

**Lewin's 3-Stage Model** (foundational):

- **Unfreeze**: Disconfirm current state; create motivation to change; psychological readiness
- **Change**: Movement toward new state; experimentation; learning
- **Refreeze**: Stabilize new state; reinforce through systems and culture

Change stalls most often in the refreeze phase when resources are diverted.

**ADKAR Model** (Prosci - individual-focused change adoption):

Individual progression through change follows predictable stages. Different people progress at different speeds. Each stage requires distinct interventions:

**Stage 1: Awareness** (Understanding the need for change)
- What: People understand why change is needed; recognize business drivers
- How to build: Communicate business case clearly; show market threats or opportunities; explain why status quo isn't viable
- Failure point: Lack of clear business case; people don't understand why change matters
- Intervention: Transparent communication about drivers; involvement in understanding the problem

**Stage 2: Desire** (Willingness to participate and support)
- What: People want to embrace the change; see personal benefit or align with values
- How to build: Connect change to personal interests; address fears directly; involve people in designing solution
- Failure point: People understand change but don't want it; see it as threatening or not relevant to them
- Intervention: Address concerns openly; show how change serves their interests; reduce fear through transparency

**Stage 3: Knowledge** (Understanding how to change; skill development)
- What: People know what to do; have capability and skills required
- How to build: Training, documentation, coaching, practice in safe environment
- Failure point: People understand need and want change but don't know how
- Intervention: Targeted training; hands-on coaching; opportunity to practice; documentation; clear processes

**Stage 4: Ability** (Demonstrating new skills and behaviors)
- What: People can execute new behaviors; muscle memory builds through practice
- How to build: Ongoing coaching; feedback; opportunity for repeated practice; peer support
- Failure point: People know what to do but can't execute consistently; reverting to old habits under stress
- Intervention: Continued coaching; reinforcement; observation and feedback; supporting removal of obstacles

**Stage 5: Reinforcement** (Sustaining the change; preventing regression)
- What: New behaviors become habitual; change embeds in systems and culture
- How to build: Recognition and rewards for new behaviors; measurement systems that reinforce; leadership modeling; consequences for old behaviors
- Failure point: Most common failure point; change initiatives fade when initial energy dissipates
- Intervention: Continuous reinforcement; measurement and feedback; celebrating success; making consequences clear for backsliding

**Key insights**:
- Change fails most at Desire (people don't want it) and Reinforcement (momentum fades)
- People progress at different rates through stages; don't assume everyone is at the same stage
- Going backward is common under stress; expected and managed rather than punishment
- Interventions must match the stage (training doesn't work if Desire is missing; communication doesn't work if Knowledge is missing)

**Understanding resistance**: Resistance is often rational—people fear loss of status, security, or identity. Effective leaders: Listen to resist, mine for legitimate concerns, involve resisters in solution design, acknowledge what will be lost.

**Building coalitions**: Change requires sponsors (have power and credibility), advocates (true believers), and participants (affected by change). Sustaining change requires moving advocates into formal authority positions.

**Sustaining change**: 70% of changes fail not because of poor design but because of insufficient attention to embedding change in: incentive systems, measurement systems, resource allocation, hiring/promotion criteria, decision-making processes, leadership behavior.
