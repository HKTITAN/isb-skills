**Definition**: Changing how a company creates, delivers, and captures value—often more powerful than product innovation.

#### Business Model Framework

**1. Value Proposition**: What problem do you solve? Unique value for whom?

**2. Value Creation**: How do you build and deliver this value?

**3. Value Delivery**: How do you reach customers? Distribution channels?

**4. Value Capture**: How do you make money? Revenue model?

#### Types of Business Model Innovation

**Subscription Models**:

- Recurring fee for continuous access
- Advantages: Predictable revenue, customer loyalty, data advantages
- Disadvantages: Customer acquisition cost recovery, churn kills growth
- Examples: Netflix, Salesforce, Spotify

**Freemium**:

- Free base product; paid premium features
- Advantages: Removes adoption barrier, natural funnel
- Disadvantages: Conversion rates critical, free tier costs money
- Examples: LinkedIn, Slack, Dropbox

**Direct-to-Consumer (DTC)**:

- Sell directly to customers; bypass middlemen
- Advantages: Higher margins, customer data, brand control
- Disadvantages: High customer acquisition costs, logistics investment
- Examples: Warby Parker, Allbirds, Tesla

**Licensing/IP**:

- License technology, brand, or intellectual property
- Advantages: Asset-light, multiple revenue streams, defensive
- Disadvantages: Quality control, cannibalization risk
- Examples: ARM Holdings, Disney, Tesla (patent licensing)

#### Digital Transformation Enablers

**Data as asset**: Data becomes product itself (insurance pricing insights, customer analytics)

**Direct access**: E-commerce and apps create direct customer relationships and data

**On-demand scaling**: Cloud infrastructure scales automatically; companies don't pre-build capacity

**AI and machine learning**: Models improve with data; personalization at scale

**Ecosystem integration**: APIs and integrations create platforms; companies embed in others' workflows
