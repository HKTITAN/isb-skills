**Definition**: How regulation of market power affects strategy and competition.

#### Monopoly Analysis

**Market power definition**: Ability to sustain prices above competitive level without losing customers

**Indicators**:

- High market share (>70% often presumed)
- Barriers to entry
- Evidence of pricing power
- Lack of substitutes

**Antitrust concerns**:

- Raised prices above competitive level
- Reduced innovation incentive
- Customer lock-in and reduced choice
- Competitive exclusion

#### Types of Anticompetitive Conduct

**Predatory pricing**: Below-cost pricing to eliminate competitors (then raise prices)

**Exclusive dealing**: Requiring customers not to buy from competitors

**Tying**: Conditioning sale of one product on purchase of another

**Refusal to deal**: Excluding competitors from essential inputs

**Platform favoritism**: Preferencing own products on platform

#### Merger Analysis

**Concerns**:

- **Horizontal mergers** (same market): Reduce competition; increase price/reduce output risk
- **Vertical mergers** (supply chain): Raise rivals' costs; foreclose competitors
- **Conglomerate mergers**: Less concern unless exclusionary practices possible

**Analysis factors**:

- Market concentration: Does merger significantly increase?
- Barriers to entry: Can new competitors offset merger effects?
- Buyer power: Do buyers discipline monopoly pricing?
- Substitutes: Close substitutes limit pricing power?
- Efficiencies: Cost reductions offsetting competitive concerns?

**Remedy options**:

- Block merger entirely
- Require divestitures (sell assets)
- Require licensing or IP sharing
- Behavioral remedies (pricing commitments, access provisions)

#### Innovation and Competition

**Competition drives innovation**:

- Competitors innovate to differentiate and gain share
- Multiple innovators increase pace
- Example: Smartphone market (Apple, Samsung, others) drove innovation

**Monopoly and innovation**:

- Patent protection enables R&D investment
- Innovation creates intellectual property monopolies
- Risk: Monopoly reduces incentive to innovate further
- Patent thickets slow follow-on innovation
