**1. Start with industry/competitive context**

- Understand industry structure and competitors first
- Company strategy makes sense only in industry context
- Different industries have different strategic requirements

**2. Map strategic choices to competitive advantage**

- Every decision should improve cost position, differentiation, focus, or competitive dynamism
- How does decision affect ROIC?
- What competitor responses are likely?
- Is advantage defensible?

**3. Use frameworks to structure thinking, not as gospel**

- Frameworks (Five Forces, Value Chain, MECE) are tools, not gospel
- Apply where they add clarity; drop if not useful
- Don't force complex reality into overly simple frameworks

**4. Consider multiple strategic options**

- Avoid anchoring to first option identified
- Develop 2-3 credible alternatives
- Evaluate each against criteria (returns, advantage, feasibility, risk)
- Compare trade-offs explicitly

**5. Connect strategy to financial outcomes**

- Ultimately, strategy succeeds if it improves financial performance
- Trace path from strategic choice to financial impact
- Example: Cost leadership should show COGS and margin improvement

**6. Assess sustainability of competitive advantage**

- What is likely lifespan of advantage?
- What could erode the advantage?
- What barriers to imitation exist?
- What is required to sustain?

**7. Think about second-order effects**

- If we lower prices to gain share, competitors will respond (price war)
- If we invest in R&D, competitors may do same (race to parity)
- Second-order effects often determine success/failure
