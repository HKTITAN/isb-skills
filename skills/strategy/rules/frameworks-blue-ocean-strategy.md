## title: "BLUE OCEAN STRATEGY"
impact: CRITICAL
impactDescription: "Core MBA framework, foundational to domain"
tags: blue, ocean, strategy

**Definition**: Creating uncontested market spaces (blue oceans) through value innovation rather than competing in existing markets (red oceans).

#### Red Ocean vs. Blue Ocean

**Red Ocean**:

- Established industry; defined rules of competition
- Competitors fight for share of existing demand
- Value-cost trade-off: cost leadership or differentiation
- Intense competition; profitability suffers

**Blue Ocean**:

- Undefined, newly created market space
- Competition is irrelevant (no direct competitors initially)
- Value innovation: achieve low cost AND differentiation
- Abundant demand; high margins

#### Value Innovation (Core Concept)

**Traditional strategic logic**:

- Choose: Cost leadership OR differentiation (trade-off)
- Compete within industry rules
- Compete against rivals

**Blue Ocean logic**:

- Pursue: Cost reduction AND differentiation (value innovation)
- Change the rules of competition
- Make competitors irrelevant

#### Four Actions Framework

Systematically identify value innovation opportunities:

**1. Eliminate** — Remove factors the industry takes for granted

- Questions: What factors should be removed? What does industry overinvest in?
- Example (Southwest): Eliminated meals, seat selection, hub-and-spoke connections
- Example (Netflix): Eliminated late fees, physical locations

**2. Reduce** — Lower factors below industry standards

- Questions: What factors should be reduced below industry norms?
- Example (Southwest): Reduced comfort (standard seats, no meals)
- Example (Cirque du Soleil): Reduced star power, animal acts

**3. Raise** — Increase factors above industry standards

- Questions: What should be raised above industry standards? What attracts non-customers?
- Example (Southwest): Raised frequency, speed, friendliness
- Example (Cirque du Soleil): Raised artistic quality, acrobatic difficulty, production value

**4. Create** — Introduce factors industry has never offered

- Questions: What entirely new factors should be created? What would attract non-customers?
- Example (Southwest): Created friendly brand personality, ticketless travel
- Example (Netflix): Created personalized recommendations, original content, no-commitment subscription

#### Strategy Canvas Tool

Visual framework for analyzing industry value factors and identifying innovation:

- Y-axis: Level of each factor (low to high)
- X-axis: Competing factors in industry
- Line: Firm's value profile across factors

**Reading the canvas**:

- **High investments**: Are customers overserved? Can factors be reduced?
- **Low investments**: Are customers underserved? Should factors be raised?
- **Missing factors**: What new factors could be created?

#### Examples of Blue Oceans

**Netflix**:

- Eliminated: Late fees, need to visit store
- Reduced: Selection breadth compared to Blockbuster
- Raised: Convenience, recommendation algorithms
- Created: Subscription model, personalization, original content
- Result: Destroyed video rental industry

**Southwest Airlines**:

- Eliminated: Meals, seat assignments, hub-and-spoke model
- Reduced: Comfort, connection reliability
- Raised: Frequency, speed, friendliness
- Created: Fun brand personality, point-to-point service
- Result: Disrupted aviation industry

**Cirque du Soleil**:

- Eliminated: Animals, celebrity stars
- Reduced: Humor, shock value
- Raised: Artistic quality, acrobatic sophistication, production artistry
- Created: Adult appeal, theatrical storytelling
- Result: Created adult entertainment category, premium pricing

#### Limitations and Critiques

- **Sustainability**: Blue oceans attract competition; eventually become red oceans
- **Market boundaries**: What's "blue" depends on perspective; boundaries subjective
- **Generalizability**: Works better for consumer/service industries
- **Implementation**: Stories compelling; guidance limited
- **Risk**: Creating new markets is riskier than competing in existing ones
