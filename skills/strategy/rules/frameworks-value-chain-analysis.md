**Definition**: A framework disaggregating firms into discrete, strategically relevant activities to understand cost structure and competitive advantage sources.

#### Primary Activities (Direct Value Creation)

**1. Inbound Logistics**
- Receiving, storing, and distributing inputs to production
- Cost drivers: batch size, distance to suppliers, inventory levels, supplier relationships
- Strategic implications: 
  - Just-in-time (JIT) reduces inventory costs but increases supply chain risk
  - Proximity to suppliers reduces transportation costs
  - Supplier relationships enable quality, innovation, cost advantages

**2. Operations**
- Manufacturing, processing, or service delivery
- Cost drivers: capacity utilization, automation level, process efficiency, standardization vs. customization
- Strategic implications:
  - Automation reduces labor costs; increases capital requirements and inflexibility
  - Standardization (mass production) vs. customization trade-off
  - Location decisions (labor costs, proximity to markets, transportation)
  - Vertical integration vs. outsourcing

**3. Outbound Logistics**
- Order processing, fulfillment, distribution to customers
- Cost drivers: warehouse locations, transportation modes, inventory management, fulfillment speed
- Strategic implications:
  - Direct-to-consumer reduces distribution costs but requires fulfillment investment
  - Multi-channel distribution creates operational complexity
  - Fulfillment center location impacts delivery speed and cost
  - Reverse logistics and returns increasingly important

**4. Marketing and Sales**
- Brand building, customer acquisition, sales force management
- Cost drivers: advertising intensity, sales force size, distribution breadth
- Strategic implications:
  - Direct sales (high touch) vs. indirect (high volume) trade-off
  - Digital marketing efficiency improving ROI on brand building
  - Pricing strategy connected to positioning
  - Customer acquisition cost (CAC) vs. lifetime value (LTV) determines sustainability

**5. Service**
- After-sales support, maintenance, customer support
- Cost drivers: service network density, response time, technical expertise requirements
- Strategic implications:
  - Service creates switching costs (lock-in through relationships)
  - Service can be source of recurring revenue
  - Service quality signals overall company quality

#### Support Activities (Enable Primary Activities)

**1. Procurement**
- Purchasing of inputs, equipment, supplies across all activities
- Strategic implications: Supplier relationships, negotiation power, quality assurance

**2. Technology Development**
- R&D, process technology, product design, IT systems
- Strategic implications: Drives innovation, operational efficiency, competitive advantage

**3. Human Resources**
- Recruitment, training, compensation, organizational development
- Strategic implications: Culture, capability development, employee retention

**4. Firm Infrastructure**
- Finance, legal, accounting, general management, strategic planning
- Strategic implications: Governance, capital structure, strategic direction

#### Margin Analysis

**Profit Margin = Total Value - Total Cost (Primary + Support Activities)**

Competitive advantage comes from:
1. **Increasing perceived value** (differentiation premium pricing)
2. **Reducing costs** (operational efficiency)
3. **Optimizing the entire value system** (not just internal chain)

#### Value System (Extended Chain)

Strategic advantage often comes from optimizing the entire value system:
```
Supplier Value Chain → Company Value Chain → Channel Value Chain → Customer Value Chain
```

**Example**: Apple's advantage comes not just from internal operations but from:
- Supply chain integration with manufacturers
- Distribution through Apple stores and online channels
- Integration with complementors (app developers)
- Customer service and ecosystem integration
