**Definition**: Three strategic positions a firm can adopt to compete: cost leadership, differentiation, or focus.

#### Cost Leadership

**Objective**: Become the lowest-cost producer in the industry while maintaining acceptable quality.

**How to achieve**:
- Economies of scale through high volume
- Process efficiency and lean operations
- Simple product design (reduces costs)
- Supply chain optimization
- Vertical integration to control inputs

**Advantages**:
- Can compete on price and still earn acceptable returns
- Protected against price wars (further than competitors)
- Bargaining power over suppliers and customers
- Resilience in downturns

**Risks**:
- Price wars erode margins for all competitors
- Sustained advantage difficult (competitors eventually match costs)
- May result in commoditization
- Risk of neglecting quality

**Examples**:
- Southwest Airlines: No frills, rapid turnaround, secondary airports
- Walmart: Supply chain efficiency, buying power, lean operations
- IKEA: Flat-pack design, self-assembly, limited selection

#### Differentiation

**Objective**: Offer unique value customers are willing to pay premium for.

**How to achieve**:
- Superior product quality or features
- Strong brand and reputation
- Exceptional customer service
- Innovation and design
- Convenience or user experience
- Sustainability or values alignment

**Advantages**:
- Can command premium prices
- Customer loyalty reduces switching
- Less vulnerable to price competition
- Higher margins

**Risks**:
- Premium pricing attracts competitors
- Differentiation must be defensible against imitation
- May have limited market size
- Imitation erodes advantage over time
- Requires continuous innovation to sustain

**Examples**:
- Apple: Design, ecosystem integration, brand prestige, user experience
- Ferrari: Performance, exclusivity, heritage, craftsmanship
- Patagonia: Environmental commitment, quality, values alignment

#### Focus Strategy

**Objective**: Serve a specific market niche better than generalists.

**Types**:
- **Cost focus**: Lowest cost for a specific segment
- **Differentiation focus**: Most differentiated for a specific segment

**Advantages**:
- Deep understanding of niche customer needs
- Less competition within niche
- Can command premium or operate at lower cost
- Specialized capabilities hard for generalists to match

**Risks**:
- Limited market size restricts growth
- Niche attractiveness may decline
- Larger competitors can enter niche
- Technology change can make niche obsolete

**Examples**:
- Rolex: Luxury watches (differentiation focus)
- Dollar Shave Club: Budget razors (cost focus)
- Salesforce: Cloud CRM software (differentiation focus in SMB segment)
