**Definition**: Strategic choices for operating across multiple countries and managing geographic diversity.

#### AAA Framework (Ghemawat)

Three generic approaches to international strategy:

**1. Adaptation (Localization)**
- Customize products and operations for local markets
- Respond to local preferences, regulations, competition

**Advantages**:
- Better fit with local customer needs
- Responsive to local competition

**Disadvantages**:
- Higher costs (lose economies of scale)
- Slower decision-making
- Difficult to leverage global capabilities

**When to pursue**: Heterogeneous customer preferences, local competition important

**Examples**: McDonald's menu localization, P&G tailoring shampoos to local hair types

**2. Aggregation (Standardization)**
- Standardize products and operations across markets
- Leverage global scale and efficiency

**Advantages**:
- Lower costs (economies of scale)
- Consistent brand and experience
- Faster decision-making

**Disadvantages**:
- May not fit local needs
- Vulnerable to competitors tailored to local preferences

**When to pursue**: Homogeneous customer preferences, global competition

**Examples**: 
- Coca-Cola: Same product globally (with some variations)
- Intel: Same processor architecture globally
- iPhone: Same device globally

**3. Arbitrage**
- Exploit differences between markets (cost, regulations, talent, resources)
- Locate activities where advantages are greatest

**Advantages**:
- Huge cost differentials (labor, manufacturing, R&D)
- Access to talent and resources
- Tax optimization

**Disadvantages**:
- Vulnerable to exchange rate fluctuations
- Supply chain complexity
- Protectionist pressures

**When to pursue**: Large cost or resource differences

**Examples**:
- Manufacturing in low-cost countries (China, Vietnam)
- R&D in talent hubs (India, Israel, China)
- Services offshoring to low-cost locations

#### CAGE Distance Framework (Ghemawat)

Understanding differences between countries helps determine international strategy.

**C: Cultural distance**
- Language, values, business practices, customer preferences
- Higher distance: More adaptation needed

**A: Administrative distance**
- Political relationship, regulatory framework, trade agreements
- Higher distance: More regulatory complexity, trade barriers

**G: Geographic distance**
- Physical distance, transportation cost, time zone differences
- Higher distance: More logistics complexity, coordination challenges

**E: Economic distance**
- Income levels, wealth, consumption patterns
- Higher distance: Different pricing, product features

**Strategy implications**:
- High CAGE distance: Greater adaptation, partnership, localization needed
- Low CAGE distance: More aggregation, direct investment, standardization

#### Entry Modes

**Exporting**:
- Manufacture at home; ship to foreign markets
- Advantages: Low investment, flexibility
- Disadvantages: Transportation costs, tariffs, limited local presence

**Licensing/Franchising**:
- License technology, brand, or business model to foreign partner
- Advantages: Low investment, capital-light, fast entry
- Disadvantages: Limited control, IP risk, quality concerns

**Joint Venture**:
- Partner with local firm to operate in market
- Advantages: Local knowledge, shared risk, regulatory access
- Disadvantages: Governance complexity, slower decisions, profit sharing

**Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**:
- Establish subsidiary in foreign market
- Advantages: Full control, long-term commitment, local capabilities
- Disadvantages: High investment, complex operations, regulatory risk
