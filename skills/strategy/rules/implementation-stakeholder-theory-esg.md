**Definition**: Strategic decision-making incorporating interests of all stakeholders, not just shareholders.

#### Shareholder Primacy vs. Stakeholder Capitalism

**Shareholder primacy**:
- Primary obligation to maximize shareholder returns
- Other stakeholders matter only if they affect shareholder value
- Capital allocation focuses on financial returns

**Stakeholder capitalism**:
- Balance interests of multiple stakeholders
- Employees, customers, communities, environment matter intrinsically
- Long-term value creation requires serving all stakeholders well
- Decisions consider broader impact

**Strategic implications**:
- Stakeholder approach often creates higher long-term value
- May reduce short-term profitability
- Requires different governance and incentive structures

#### ESG Framework

**Environmental**:
- Carbon emissions and climate impact
- Energy efficiency
- Waste and circular economy
- Water usage and pollution
- Resource depletion

**Social**:
- Labor practices (wages, working conditions)
- Health and safety
- Community impact
- Diversity and inclusion
- Data privacy and security

**Governance**:
- Board composition and diversity
- Executive compensation alignment
- Business ethics and compliance
- Transparency and reporting
- Risk management

**Strategic importance**:
- Investor interest in ESG (cost of capital affected)
- Regulation tightening
- Customer and employee preferences
- Long-term risk management

#### Triple Bottom Line

**Three dimensions**:
- **Profit**: Financial performance
- **People**: Social impact, employee welfare
- **Planet**: Environmental sustainability

**Strategic application**:
- Identify ways to create value on all three dimensions
- Avoid false trade-offs (can often create both profit and social value)
- Measure and report across all dimensions

#### Shared Value Creation (Porter & Kramer)

**Concept**: Creating economic value while addressing societal challenges

**Approach**:
1. Identify societal challenge affecting business
2. Develop business solution addressing challenge AND creating value
3. Business profit and social good advance together

**Examples**:
- Nestlé farmer training: Improves farmer income; improves supply chain quality
- TATA Steel community programs: Social impact; reduces labor disputes
- Unilever sustainable brands: Environmental/social focus; growing faster
