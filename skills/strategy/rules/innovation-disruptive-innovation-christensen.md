**Definition**: Innovation that disrupts incumbent markets by serving lower-end or new customers with simpler, cheaper products.

#### Disruptive vs. Sustaining Innovation

**Sustaining innovation**:
- Improves existing products for existing customers
- Examples: Faster computers, better cameras, lighter phones
- Incumbents win (they control distribution and customer relationships)

**Disruptive innovation**:
- Simpler, cheaper product for lower-end or new customers
- Performance gap with incumbent products (initially)
- Improves over time; eventually outperforms
- Incumbents lose (locked into high-margin, high-performance positioning)

#### The Innovator's Dilemma

**The problem**: Disruptive innovations are often uneconomical for incumbents to pursue

**Why**:
- Low margins in new markets (far smaller than incumbents' profitable core)
- Threatens existing business (cannibalizes revenues)
- Doesn't fit customer needs of high-margin segment
- Rational decision-making favors ignoring disruptive threat initially

**Result**: Incumbents dismiss disruptive threats; competitors win

#### Classic Disruptive Examples

**Steel industry**:
- Integrated mills (incumbents) focused on high-margin, high-volume production
- Mini-mills (disruptors) started on simple products (rebar), low cost
- Mini-mills progressively improved; eventually competed on all products
- Integrated mills lost market share and profitability

**Disk drives**:
- 14" drives dominated
- 8" drives emerged (disruptive)
- 5.25" drives emerged (disruptive)
- 3.5" drives emerged (disruptive)
- Each time, incumbents dismissed; each time, disruption eventually dominated

**Photography**:
- Film photography (Kodak) dominated
- Digital photography emerged (simple, low-cost initially)
- Image quality gap initially favored film
- Digital improved; eventually superior
- Kodak invented digital but couldn't embrace it (threatened film business)
- Digital completely displaced film

#### Disruption Pattern

1. Disruptive product enters at low end (low performance, low price)
2. Incumbents ignore (not profitable, not for their customers)
3. Disruption improves over time (learning curves, innovation)
4. Eventually matches incumbent performance at lower cost
5. Disruption captures market share; incumbents decline

#### How Incumbents Can Respond

**Embrace the disruptive model**:
- Launch separate division (not constrained by core business)
- Different management, metrics, P&L
- Target new market, not existing customers

**Redefine "good enough"**:
- Focus on what matters to customers
- Disruptive products often outperform where it matters

**Position for adjacent markets**:
- Where disruption enters, follow eventually
- Anticipate trajectory; prepare to defend new market
