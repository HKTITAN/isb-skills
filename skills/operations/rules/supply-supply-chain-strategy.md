**End-to-End Perspective**: Optimize entire chain from raw materials through final delivery, not individual functions. Recognize that optimizing one node (e.g., procurement cost) may create problems downstream.

**Push vs. Pull Systems**:

- Push: Production based on forecasts; efficient for stable demand but risks excess inventory and forecasting errors
- Pull: Production triggered by actual demand; responsive but requires fast information and short lead times
- Hybrid: Push for stable base demand; pull for variability above base (two-bin approach)

**Responsive vs. Efficient Supply Chains**:

- Responsive: High flexibility, fast response, higher cost; for fashion, electronics with short lifecycles, low volume
- Efficient: Optimized for cost, standardized products, longer lead times; for commodities, stable demand, high volume
- Choice depends on product lifecycle, demand variability, margin
- Many companies maintain both: responsive for trendy items, efficient for basics

**Vertical Integration vs. Outsourcing**: 

- Integration: Control, proprietary knowledge, but high capital, inflexibility, distraction from core competency
- Outsourcing: Lower capital, flexibility, cost, but quality risk, control loss, supply dependency
- Hybrid: Outsource non-core; maintain control of proprietary/strategic elements

**Centralization vs. Decentralization of Inventory**: 

- Centralized: Single warehouse reduces safety stock (pooling effect, reduced variance), simplified management, but increases distribution costs and delivery time
- Decentralized: Multiple warehouses near customers, faster delivery, but higher total safety stock and complexity
- Pooling benefit: If demands are independent, σ_total = σ / √n (variance reduces with pooling)

**Supply Chain Coordination Mechanisms**:

- Collaborative Planning, Forecasting, Replenishment (CPFR): Joint planning across companies
- Vendor-Managed Inventory (VMI): Supplier manages customer inventory based on actual consumption
- Information Sharing: Share point-of-sale data, inventory levels with upstream partners to reduce bullwhip
- Stable Pricing: Consistent prices prevent demand surges from promotions
- Consignment Inventory: Supplier owns goods until customer uses them (reduces buyer capital)
