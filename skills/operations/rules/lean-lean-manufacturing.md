## title: "Lean Manufacturing"
impact: HIGH
impactDescription: "Important concept frequently tested in cases"
tags: finance, lean, manufacturing, marketing, operations, technology

Lean is a systematic approach to identifying and eliminating waste while maximizing customer value.

**Seven Wastes (TIMWOODS)**:

1. Transport: Unnecessary movement of materials
2. Inventory: Excess WIP hiding problems and tying up capital
3. Motion: Unnecessary worker movements
4. Waiting: Work stopped due to unavailable resources or unbalanced lines
5. Overproduction: Making more than immediately needed
6. Over-processing: Adding quality/features beyond customer requirements
7. Defects: Quality failures requiring rework and scrap
8. Skills (added): Not fully utilizing worker knowledge and improvement suggestions

**Just-In-Time (JIT)**: Produce or procure exactly what is needed, exactly when needed. Requires reliable suppliers, short setup times, strong quality control.

**Kaizen**: Continuous improvement philosophy—all workers suggest small improvements; many small improvements compound over time into transformative results.

**5S Framework**:

1. Sort: Remove unnecessary items from work area
2. Set in Order: Organize remaining items for easy access (visual controls, labels, shadows)
3. Shine: Clean and inspect equipment to identify problems
4. Standardize: Establish standard procedures and documentation
5. Sustain: Maintain discipline through culture and accountability

**Value Stream Mapping**: Visual representation of all activities (value-adding and non-value-adding) required to produce product/service. Identifies waste and improvement opportunities.

**Pull vs. Push Systems**: Pull (e.g., kanban) produces only when downstream demand exists, reducing inventory. Push produces based on forecast, more efficient for stable demand but risks excess inventory.

**Kanban**: Visual control system using cards/signals to authorize production. Limits WIP, creates transparency, pulls based on actual consumption. Number of cards limits maximum WIP.

**Continuous Improvement Culture (Kaizen)**: Embed improvement in job descriptions; celebrate small wins; make process improvement everyone's responsibility; psychological safety to speak up.
