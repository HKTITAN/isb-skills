## title: "Service Operations Characteristics"
impact: HIGH
impactDescription: "Important concept frequently tested in cases"
tags: characteristics, operations, quantitative, service

Service operations fundamentally differ from manufacturing:

- **Perishability**: Cannot inventory services—if barista makes latte with no order, output is lost. Capacity unused is lost forever (unlike inventory)
- **Intangibility**: Output is not physical; harder to assess quality before purchase; requires clear communication
- **Variability**: Human interaction introduces variability; harder to standardize than manufactured goods
- **Simultaneity**: Production and consumption happen at same time; customer present during service delivery

**Service Design**: Starbucks manages operation through:

- Physical space (register layout, queue design, throughput)
- Process design (step sequence: order → payment → preparation → delivery)
- Technology (mobile ordering reduces in-store queues, enables advance preparation)
- Staff training (empowerment to handle exceptions)

**Customer Contact Levels**:

- Low-contact: Insurance claims processing, data entry; standardized, easily automated
- High-contact: Consulting, healthcare, education; needs clear core processes plus staff trained for variation and customization
- Implication: High-contact services can't fully standardize without losing customer value

**Service Failures and Recovery**: 

- Service failures happen; recovery determines if customer is retained or lost
- Importance: Customer dissatisfaction from recovery failure often larger than from original failure
- Solutions: Clear failure-handling processes, empowered staff (no escalation for simple recovery), quick resolution, follow-up
- Psychology: Recovery attempt itself (not perfection) often rebuilds trust if done well

**Service Capacity Planning**:

- Unlike manufacturing, can't build inventory to smooth demand
- Must match capacity to demand patterns (staffing levels, hours of operation)
- Overbooking strategy: Airlines, hotels accept more reservations than capacity to account for no-shows
- Yield management: Price variation by demand time (hotels, airlines) to balance occupancy and price
