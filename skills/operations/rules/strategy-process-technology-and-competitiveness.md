**Job Shop vs. Mass Production**:

Job Shop:
- Small volumes, high variety, customer-specific
- Flexible, general-purpose equipment
- Skilled workers; high labor costs
- Long lead times, high cost per unit

Mass Production:
- High volumes, low variety, standardized
- Specialized equipment (difficult to retool)
- Less-skilled workers; lower labor costs
- Fast throughput, low cost per unit
- Capital-intensive; inflexible

**Automation and Technology Choices**:
- **Full Automation**: High capital, low labor, inflexible (good for high-volume, stable demand)
- **Flexible Automation**: Programmable systems (robots, CNC), adjust for product variety, medium capital
- **Fixed Automation**: Single-purpose, lowest cost per unit at high volume, can't retool
- **No Automation**: High labor, flexible, low capital (good for low-volume, customization)
- **Assessment Framework**: Compare Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) of alternatives across expected production volumes and product variety needs

**Make-vs-Buy Decision**:
- **Make**: Control, proprietary knowledge, capacity utilization, but capital, complexity, focus dilution
- **Buy (Outsource)**: Lower capital, flexibility, access to expertise, but quality risk, dependency, less control
- **Hybrid**: Outsource commodity items; make strategically important/proprietary components

Technology choice fundamentally shapes competitive capability. "Best" depends on strategic objectives, volumes, and flexibility needs.
