Quality means meeting specifications consistently and meeting customer expectations.

**Quality Cost Framework**: Total Quality Cost = Prevention Costs + Appraisal Costs + Failure Costs
- **Prevention Costs**: Training, controls, testing, process design, supplier management (upfront investment to prevent defects)
- **Appraisal Costs**: Inspection, testing, audits (costs to detect defects)
- **Failure Costs**: Rework, scrap, warranty, returns, customer dissatisfaction, legal liability (costs of defects that reach customer)
- **Optimal Quality**: NOT zero defects; where marginal improvement cost = marginal failure cost savings. Typically requires 3-4 sigma capability
- **Insight**: Investing in prevention is usually cheaper than fixing failures; optimal quality is often higher quality than naive thinking suggests
- **Hidden costs**: Customer dissatisfaction, lost repeat business, reputation damage often larger than direct failure costs

**Control Charts and Statistical Process Control (SPC)**: Track variation over time; distinguish normal variation from signs of problems. When points exceed control limits or trends appear, investigate and correct.

**Process Capability Indices**:
- Cp = (USL - LSL) / (6σ): measures capability relative to specification width
- Cpk = min[(USL - mean)/(3σ), (mean - LSL)/(3σ)]: accounts for process centering
- Cpk > 1.33 typically considered capable
