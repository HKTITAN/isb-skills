**Capacity Decision Strategies** (multi-year commitments):
- **Lead**: Build ahead of demand (never lose sales due to capacity, but excess capacity costs high)
- **Match**: Build to meet current demand (efficient asset utilization, balanced risk)
- **Lag**: Build after demand emerges (minimal excess, but initial constraints and lost sales)
- Choice depends on: Market growth rate, capital costs, production speed, competitive intensity

**Facility Location Factors**:
1. **Proximity to suppliers**: Lower input costs, shorter lead times, better quality control
2. **Proximity to customers**: Lower logistics costs, faster delivery, better service responsiveness
3. **Labor availability and costs**: Availability of skilled workers, wage levels, productivity, turnover
4. **Infrastructure and utilities**: Power, water, transportation (rail, ports, highways), quality
5. **Regulatory environment**: Tariffs, local content requirements, environmental regulations, labor laws
6. **Incentives**: Tax abatements, subsidies, grants from local government
7. **Strategic positioning**: Access to regional markets, distribution networks, future growth

**Centralized vs. Distributed Networks**:
- **Centralized**: Economies of scale (fewer facilities, higher utilization), simplified management, inventory pooling, but long supply lines and slow response
- **Distributed**: Proximity to customers (faster delivery, higher service level), supply resilience, but diseconomies of scale and higher total inventory
- **Hybrid**: Often optimal (e.g., central manufacturing + regional distribution centers)
